Film Review

Chinatown

The Tragedy of Quality and Time
Director Roman Polanski
Year 1974
Starring Jack Nicholson, Faye Dunaway, John Huston

Forget it, Jake.
It's Chinatown.

Pardon the prolonged summer break, o' dearest reader. But even my mighty digital pen has suffered the tragic consequences of the viral sin known as sloth. A slothing largely caused by lack of mind-tingling, heart-pumping movies, in this long Norwegian summer.

A lack of stimuli that could only be satiated by going back in the time lane, to a time that was unfortunately skipped by my frivolous past. That is the time of Polanski's reign of fire in the noir market. An old-timey time, that sees fedora-sporting investigators protagonist of tornadoing twists, and as noteworthy tornadoes of bitchslaps. Discontinued school of Freud's bitchslaps. Miss ya.

That is the Time of Chinatown.

✦

The movie's premise is all about adultery; quite the ironic premonitory tale. But that's just the colorful butterfly to the effect and effective tale of Chinatown.

Just a few snippets of retro lewdness before we're introduced to Jake Gittes, better known as giga-charismatic Jack Nicholson. Aside from charming unisex underwear off of viewers, Jake spends day and night in search of delight. A private investigator with a knack for adultery cases, he's soon enough tasked with a case that will go way beyond his duties, or his ethos. The infamous water commissioner case. Gotta love the '70s.

In the middle of a drought, the water commissioner drowns. Ain't that something.

✦

And with just this bit of a push, all of a sudden Robert Towne's play of themes starts narrating itself. A beautiful play on our understanding of coincidence and the struggle to unpuzzle the mysteries hiding behind very thin veils. This is what is most effectively captured by Polanski, with a Nicholson that goes beyond his duties to fulfill a moral choice.

But this is Chinatown, a town where rights and wrongs are dictated by the will of the money-strong. This Chinatown, often reminded of by the many characters that have escaped it, is more than the MacGuffin it is presented to be. A small town that englobes the rules of the world.

Forget it, Jake, it's Chinatown.

✦

A Chinatown filled with melancholy, as the damaged Jake is sent off by the end of his purposeless journey. A Chinatown that is also one of the better neo-noirs, and fortunately avoids me the troubles of giving further detail, as I sip on my 10 bucks whiskey.

Chinatown is truly one of the movies that can't but just make you fall in love with cinema — if not just renew a love that may be fading. It may otherwise make you ooze with liquid passion for the beautiful art of acting, with what is yet another brilliant performance by the great Jack Nicholson.

✦

What has to be said though, with an adverb that pains my heart and typing fingers, is that it may and will struggle to entice a mind ripened with 3-second video stimuli.

That's because Chinatown is truly old-timey. Old-timey in execution, old-timey in narration, and old-timey in women's rights and lefts. A time when bitch-slapping bitches was not just a Detroit pimp schtick, but a universal pastime. If not for Robert Towne's screenplay, one that time bends and smoothly navigates with its timeless narrations of power, injustice, and love.

But it MUST have been revolutionary at the time, I guess. Sheesh, I'm an old, old man. But luckily, this is Chinatown, so let's all forget it.
