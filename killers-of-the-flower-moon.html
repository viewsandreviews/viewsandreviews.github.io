Film Review

Killers of the Flower Moon

Fugazi
Director Martin Scorsese
Year 2023
Starring Leonardo DiCaprio, Lily Gladstone, Robert De Niro

What is to survive without ethos?
What is to die for the cause?
In the end, all that remains are the fragrances of the Earth.

That's what an 80-year-old Scorsese — with no lost mental acumen, nor lost joy for storytelling — is here to show us. The legendary director does not surprise solely for his continued quality, one that catapults Killers of the Flower Moon to the top of the yearly charts. Even in his ripe boomerness, Scorsese shows narrative progress, as he showers in unexplored waters and tribes.

✦

It is the waters of the Osage. Waters, and land, that still remain pure in the romantic minds of the Caucasian man — until contaminated with the coarse, black smell of oil and death.

A small Native American tribe, now resident of the barren lands of Oklahoma. The Now is a post-WWI America — one that is as industrious as it is cunning. One where all must learn to trick, or inevitably end up tricked. The Osage, unfamiliar to the new settlers' trick-or-treat traditions, are the victims of all kinds of tricks. Tricked out of their newfound wealth via oil reserves recently discovered in their vast territories. Just when they were finally allowed to the Monopoly table — allowed to buy all the properties and riches that come with it — before being taught the most important rule: when you buy the well, you get all the snakes that come with it.

✦

The biggest and baddest snake is Robert De Niro's William King Hale. Guardian to most of the Osage of the ranch we are settled in — a guardian that they were forced by law to have. But guarding is not what W.K. Hale is here for, as he makes clear from the start. He's here to observe, plan, and deliver all kinds of murders of the natives who put their blissfully innocent trust in him.

Like all the best schemers, he's not here to dirty himself. His schemes involve several collaborators, mostly of the extra-chromosome kind. Among these: Earnest (Leonardo DiCaprio). Earnest not only by name, but also by ironic twist of fate, from start to end. Leo does an amalgamate of all his recent performances, which results in a glue-eating Brando impression. But that glue is what is needed, as his Earnest captures the essence of the bad-by-guilt-of-unintelligence guy.

✦

He is juxtaposed with his future lover, Mollie Kyle, played by a brilliant Lily Gladstone. Mollie is a full-blooded Osage, and that blood comes with a lot of financial perks. Earnest tries to get into the celibate Osage's grace, under pressure from his uncle. But he's not after her riches, exclusively. The pair develops a mutual, passionate love for each other. A love that is no stronger than the white man's desire for wealth. Mollie's family grows smaller, one death at a time — but her love for her husband, her naive trust, prevent her from seeing the culprits standing right in front of her.

✦

The story of Killers of the Flower Moon is a turbulent tragedy. One that does not slow in its engrossment even at the three-hour mark. One that invigorates Scorsese to take an uncharacteristic firm moral stance. We know who is bad, and who is right. We know everything, yet we understand nothing.

Incomprehensible is not what Scorsese delivers — cause he delivers. Incomprehensible is how everyone's idealistic moral compass cannot stand the rule of the strong. He who survives is what remains outside the boundaries of justice. That's the American dream.

But not all is lost, as Scorsese's public denounce is a sign of counter-movement, however unuseful it may be. One that may spark very dim lights of hope, but at least bring us to think out loud. It is not who has the right — it is what must survive, and how can it thrive.

✦

Moral diatribe is not all Scorsese has to offer, although the rest is more habitual for the great director. Everything is up to the standard he set himself, equalled by few of his peers. He brings out the best from all of his standard and non-standard crew, with performances that transport us back in time and keep us in our uncomfortable seats.

Another classic thus enters his filmography, which hopefully will continue to expand over the next few years. Because nobody will ever get tired of hearing his stories. Whether they're real, or just fairy dust.

Fugazi — adj. Fake, unauthentic, substandard person or thing. "Yo those gold chains ain't real son, they're fugazi."
