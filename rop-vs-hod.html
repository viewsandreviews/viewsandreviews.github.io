Ring of Power vs House of Dragon

Battle of the Bastardization

"The house always wins,
but one may lose the house."
— Some guy named Bezos or something.

Boy oh boy, here's my personal reminder of why I've always struggled to build any kind of affection to a TV series.

Some of them are like those good friends you have and have accompanied you for years. Giggles and gaggles for ages, with a few, very seldom clashes of humor, that leave just trace amounts of debris, lost in a sea of nostalgic tenderness. Others are like those friends of a friend you were introduced to during that one accidental change of plan. Those you knew for a long time, but wish you never did. Those who you may have been recommended by dears, out of compassion, to only remain in memories as regrets. Those whose fastidious stings of unnecessary dialogue bring out the Dahmer in you (I'm so good at foreshadowing). Or just those whose unappeal is such that they're nothing but shades, and in the worst of cases: nightmares.

Childhood, ruined (?)

✦

RoP, by the previously mostly successful Amazon studios, is much of the second, while also being nothing at all. Such is the level of nothingness of a show with a budget on par to Suriname's GDP that I can't, for the minimal effort I put, recall anything of substance about even the most recent of episodes. A story that could have been so much, but leaves with not even crumbles. A story where each episode becomes a chore, each new character a name to forget.

All I recall of this luxurious bore is a poorly scripted and acted princess of elves spending the majority of mid-season 1 preoccupying about usurping the thrones of most annoying character of all time. Maybe I judged you too harshly Ms. White. And yes, I'd love some breakfast.

Nothing more. Nothing more is left in my recollection and nothing more shall be added, as this bitter memory is at last, and second chances are rarer than fraudulent bankers going to jail.

✦

Less severe is my judgment on HoD, much more clever in its execution, by smartly removing any cleverness and sticking to their most successful past takes. After compiling a long, very long list of lessons learned from the (un)imaginable failed pre-sequel's ending, the screenwriters do almost their best at salvaging the dynasty of dragons.

All of HoD's characters can be Venn-diagrammed to its predecessor, not only on a family-tree-based key but from an authorship unoriginal point of view. The story brings some of the best that the GoT had to offer, moderately accelerating on bloodiness, while putting to shame both GoT and Alabama natives with their Targaryen affairs.

✦

Whispers passing through banquets of drums are more exhilarating than whatever it is RoP is talking about. 200 birth scenes more engaging than the hobbit-less non-Middle Earth. Even the lengthier bureaucratic diatribe has more zest than RoP's flavorless water soup of a story.

But that's it, the comparison that gives an alright series a triumphant victory. A medal of participation wrapped in an opaque foil of gold. Like a broke rapper's grillz.

Congratulations. Enjoy your victories, for the time being. Something I never understood, as being is the one thing time takes away. As it will most likely take away this little light of hope I have for these white-haired people to not suffer the same fate as their incestuous grandchildren.
