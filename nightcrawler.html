Film Review

Nightcrawler

Psychopacy Manifest
Director Dan Gilroy
Year 2014
Starring Jake Gyllenhaal, Rene Russo, Bill Paxton

Lou Bloom. The creep with a charm.
The Dracula of LA.
Not.

Dan Gilroy, or the 21st century Michael Cimino as I prefer calling him, I salute you. Explains the career of Christopher Walken.

A reverential bow is in order for the tragic one-time wonder, as I revisit past memorials of my troubled psyche. A bow that is to be shared with Jake Gyllenhaal. And while the latter may be far from a one-hit wonder, Nightcrawler remains his gold medal in the years of the Jake Gyllenhaanassaince. The shining star of the holy triad co-podiumed by Prisoners and Nocturnal Animals. The one that let us forgive the sins committed by Southpaw.

✦

An improbable pitch it must have been, that of Nightcrawler. One where the title doesn't mislead to the least, as we follow the nocturnal, high-speed, and high-adrenaline exploits of one Lou Bloom.

Lou Bloom. The creep with a charm. The Dracula of LA. Not. That's just what he tries to be, as this morally ambiguous video journalist endeavors to make it into the big league of freelance journalism, with nothing but a camera and rehearsed monologues. What he is instead is a perfectly uncomfortable representation of a psychopath that truly doesn't care. One whose lack of empathy cannot be compensated by any level of verbal mastery. Charming from the safety of a screen, he's the one you would not trust to clean your toilets, let alone keep you informed on the local crime news.

And it's (mostly) all within Bloom that Nightcrawler stands out, as Gyllenhaal fully embodies the character's eerie intensity.

✦

In a sea of overtly charismatic faux psycho killers, he's the one to mold the perfect psychopath whose apathetic obsessiveness is so well acted that it avoids veneration by teenage adolescents all over the world. One whose twisted world is less misunderstood than feared. One that may be not as quotable as colleagues Patrick Bateman or Alex Delarge, but one with a much creepier aura, akin to the likes of Hannibal Lecter.

✦

But enough fellatio for Mr. Gyllenhaal, as he probably received copious amounts from the late Heath Ledger. Let's go back to giving credit where it is due, as credit is the one thing Mr. Gilroy will probably never receive again. His cinematography, his dialogues, his everything is crafted to maintain the highest level of suspense. Like that one occurring tragedy that keeps you glued to the screen. Something bad is going to happen. And we all must, not-so-reluctantly, observe. We must know.

It is with relentless pacing, kept from start to bottom, that we're tasked to critique the ethical dilemmas surrounding journalism, and self-critique our lust for confirmation bias support.

✦

All of this is what makes Nightcrawler a new-age gem, whose appreciation can only grow further as time passes, and whose cult status can only be stopped by certain alien extermination.

"Is this the planet that allowed the filming of Southpaw? Die, you scum!"
