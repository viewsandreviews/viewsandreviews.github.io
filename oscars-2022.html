Oscars 2022 Round-Up

Nightmare Alley · West Side Story · Don't Look Up · King Richard

A bunch of movies with enough qualities
to resist pressing the turn off button too eagerly —
just so you can regret your decision making 2 to 3 hours later.

Here it is, the one no one was looking for, yours truly included.

EDIT: I had shelved this mashup of reviews out of complete boredom and negligence, but I've been slapped back into action with a Fresh Prince styled bitch slap as legitimate as one of Maury's DNA tests.

As such, allow me to change the future tenses of my past draft into late and forgotten past tenses. Just like this year's most awaited and pretentious cinema award ceremony has been, I'm here to present you a bunch of movies with enough qualities to resist pressing the turn off button too eagerly, just so you can regret your decision making 2 to 3 hours later.

United by belonging to the prestigious, albeit perennially decreasing in appeal, Best Movie shortlist, little is out there to stop me from harshly sending personal judgement upon these. Although valid candidates such as Drive My Car and CODA are still on my catch-up list, this personal selection of hot pics has been carefully hand-picked as the least likely front runners, but with enough variety in genre and entertainment to not qualify them for this blog's Razzie awards.

So let's get this show started, just so I can accelerate the lowering of the digital curtains.

✦

Nightmare Alley: Del Toro at his most formal albeit forgettable self. The director navigates his obsession and secret fetish for the monsterization of the human in this beautifully shot and cinematographed noir, with lately screen-shy Bradley Cooper being his Guinea Pig of choice. Still, this gloomy Alley lacks the genuine fright and thrill that characterized the director's best works, culminating in a forgettable and predictable conclusion.

Stick to fucking fish, Guillermo. Fucking. Fish.

✦

West Side Story: Spielberg showing what directing is all about: appealing to old farts with recycled and slightly revamped stories from the time they could still get it up from seeing an unveiled ankle.

JK, it's actually quite alright. And while the Story may not bring much novelty to the table, the rest of the West Side is rich, colorful and exuberantly choreographed. Of particular note the director's hilarious attempt at trying to attract zoomers, by using all possible SJW tropes, with a standout performance of an all-new ex-woman with more superpowers than Superman and Betty White combined.

✦

Don't Look Up: Continuing the tradition of exposés composed by brilliant ensembles, in similar fashion to Spotlight and The Big Short, Don't Look Up amps up on the (attempted) humour while coming up short on all other fronts. As subtle as Bill Cosby at a chloroform seminar, DLU does have a few redeemable moments, but it is all lost in its hilariously logorrheic attempt at virtue signalling.

Also, don't tell me where to look. If I wanna look at Leo's flabby, environmentally sustainable butt, I'll look at Leo's environmentally sustainable butt.

✦

King Richard: The not-so-fresh prince continues his honorable but miserable attempt at capturing gold. EDIT: well, not so miserable after all, as his golden statuette finally arrives, with award judges intimidated by his hi-five skills applied to Chris Rock's beautiful cheeks.

Unfortunately for the Williams sisters and everyone else involved in this more than satisfactory and uplifting family drama, King Richard will always be remembered as the movie that got out-staged by the worst staged performance at an Academy Award ceremony.

✦

Tadaa, that's it folks. And until the overlords enthrall us with an all-new dose of panem et circenses, I shall come back to you with a modicum of procrastination to share my thoughts on the Grand Prize winner (CODA), and his weird, foreign step-cousin (Drive My Car).
