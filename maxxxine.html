Film Review

MaXXXine

Never Judge a Book by Its Cover, or a Person by Their Eyebrows
Director Ti West
Year 2024
Starring Mia Goth, Kevin Bacon, Giancarlo Esposito

Never judge a book by its cover,
or a person by their eyebrows.
Smash, next question.

Never judge a book by its cover, or a person by their eyebrows. This was a lesson I learnt early on, thanks to my father's follicular deficiencies. That's a lesson that was refreshed by the one Mia Goth, the all-time wild card in any game of F, marry, kill. You're gonna tell me she's never stabbed someone with a pencil? Smash, next question.

The rising star returns as one of the halves of her duo performance, to conclude this gem of a trilogy. Muse of director Ti West, she is undoubtedly the key reason for the success of the horror series. Goth returns as Maxine, the character she was introduced as in the first of the trio, X.

✦

In an era prior to OnlyFans, Maxine continues her path as an aspiring porn starlet, not discouraged by the dismembering that occurred in her recent past. Now a more established name in the industry, she is ready for the switch to mainstream acting. But the realisation of the challenges that her past promiscuity will have on her future dreams will come soon to persecute her — in the most literal of ways.

Ti West's grand finale (?) continues his homage piece to the Hollywood of the past, this time with a more direct letter of devious devotion, à la Once Upon a Time in Hollywood. The director flashes colours and molassed splatter, bowing to the cinema of the '80s — which is also the setting of the film. While brilliantly managing to re-animate another subgenre of horror, the director doesn't fail to prolong his successful streak, with a story as captivating as it is simple.

✦

Maxine is soon revealed to be the other side of a shiny Hollywood coin, where the previous head was shown in Pearl and Goth's performance as the homonymous character. The two films result in direct branches from the first, showing the what-if scenarios that are often realities of a career in Hollywood.

"Turn into a monster before the monsters swallow you whole." That is the paraphrased advice Maxine receives from on-screen director Elizabeth Bender, the one to give Maxine her big break in the dreamland. An advice she does not shy away from following, and takes to her beating heart. The only one beating by the end of the runtime. Spoiler alert.

✦

Possibly on the weaker end of quality if compared to its predecessors, MaXXXine still succeeds in surviving the inevitable game of juxtaposition, ending the series on a positive note of devilish commentary. Not much more needs to be said about the film, if not to highlight the excitingly exuberant performances by Kevin Bacon and Giancarlo Esposito's toupee.

I bet it was made with Mia Goth's missing brows. Smash, next question.
