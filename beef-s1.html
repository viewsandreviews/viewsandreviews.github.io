TV Review

Beef

Season 1
Creator Lee Sung Jin
Year 2023
Starring Ali Wong, Steven Yeun, Joseph Lee

That mustache alone
could feed 1000 kulaks.
And this Beef needs no extra sauce.

My oh my. Welcome to the decade of the dragon. Or tiger. Or whichever animal dictates your go-to oriental seasonal stereotypes. Cus here I am, once again. Applauding my neighbours from the lands of the sweet and sour. The tangy and spicy. Being a PoC truly gives me infinite creative freedom. Expect me to exhaust it all in food puns.

✦

An old-school beef, dusting terminology only terminally ill millennials may still be familiar with. The beef, the feud, the archrivalry, the renaissance. That's what it's all about in this all-Asian-casted, one-season sensation, co-lead by Amy (Ali Wong) and Danny (Steven Yeun).

The two are at the opposite spectrum of the Asian scale, with neither achieving the ultimate title only a few selected like yours truly can be administered: The Doctor. After 30 years, I've been finally reunited with my father.

✦

Amy is by all accounts a more than successful lifestyle entrepreneur, albeit forever under a boiling shadow cast by her Nepo husband. Danny, by both real and falsified accounts, is nothing but an Asian disgrace. A trick by all means except the sexual kind, Danny resorts to any odd job to make means-end. But the end never seems to come.

And while seppuku is not a touched theme, honour, family obligations and death are all explored visually and aesthetically. Subtly and Kurosawa's Asian-ly.

✦

Beef is as actual as it is poignant. At the top and at the bottom. The boiling point may differ for the material substrates that compose society, but the ultimate reaction is the same: evaporation of common sense. Logic and ethics; particles blown away by a wind of unfortunate events of the Asian kind. A crescendo of rage and anxiety, previously only achieved by the likes of Adam Sandler in Uncut Gems, or Adam Sandler fans watching any of his other recent works.

✦

Still, this is no A5 marbling type-a meal. Beef arguably functions at its best in its comedy-induced, chain-reactionary storytelling more so than any other sophisticated attempts at depth. Not to deeply critique any lack of inner flavor; its seasoning is simply best positioned at surface level.

But don't let it stop you from consuming this succulent single-portion platter, as the price (time) tag is more than adequate for the offered experience.

Puns off, Nabin's out.
