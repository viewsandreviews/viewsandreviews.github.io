Film Review

The Batman

A Tainted Noir of Bottomless Grime
Director Matt Reeves
Year 2022
Starring Robert Pattinson, Paul Dano, Zoë Kravitz

A fresh-faced, mortal more than ever, fallible Batman —
Reeves confirms his talent at turning larger-than-life characters
into sympathetic humans.

Boy oh boy. If I tell you — all five of you, my hardcore readers — tell you of the uncontainable grin of excitement I sported midway through my last evening's viewing of The Batman.

Although the struggle to strap myself to the cinema seat was made easier as the final act rolled by, and that shimmering light in my coffee-stained smile slowly died down, I was a happy man. Happy to see that I didn't lose my passion for blockbuster movies, thanks to this comic book busting Batman — more reminiscent of those nostalgic, classic noir that in no way were part of my blossoming, and yet feel so vivid.

A classic Orson Wellian on crack, this new and improved Batman takes everything there is to take from the most brilliant crime thrillers and mixes it up with stunning comic book bravado and action.

✦

Robert Pattinson is the latest in a long list of caped crusaders, yet still the most doubted one before release. Pat the Bat quickly disproves the public's distrust in his ability to turn his past vampire into a credible Bat-Man, more than capable of interchanging and intertwining both his bat and man personas. A fresh-faced, mortal more than ever, and fallible Batman — this is the design of Matt Reeves' new magnum opus, who confirms his talent at turning larger-than-life characters into sympathetic humans.

The initial bleakness of the movie, which almost feels insurmountable to the point of obnoxiousness, ends up paradoxically working in its favour to make Reeves' movie a blissful ride, with excitement to spare.

✦

The Batman is not just an incredible showing of the titular character's new look, but a fabled narration of an eerily modern Gotham, filled with innumerable thrilling characters. Second in this stellar line-up is Paul Dano's enigmatic Riddler, which serves as the perfect villain to min-max the overstimulated nerves of this roller-coaster ride. A villain who is left elusive and enigmatic for most of the show, with only brief ciphers sporadically distributed to unveil his motives.

And motives he has plenty — with a story that progressively not only explains how parallel an experience he had to his counterpart Bruce Wayne, but further constructs the building blocks for a fleshed-out city of darkness, corruption and violence.

His story is only one of the threads of this finely sewn feature, which does a great job at not over-cumbering the viewer for most of its running. And during each scene where you think the thrill is about to blow off, another candle of enthusiasm is lit.

✦

Now to the bad news. It is true, as I anticipated, that this bat-smelling candle almost dies out in the final act. Arguably unneeded and logorrheic, those last 25 minutes do serve as a positive reminder of how fallible, fragile and emotive this Batman is — but also as an unenthusiastic keepsake of big-studio mandated needs for comic book movies and serialization.

But as I take out this Warner Bros signed dagger from my bleeding heart, I cherish those two hours of eye-gluing entertainment they have gifted me, as I look for tutorials to paint my eyes vanta black for my next Halloween.
