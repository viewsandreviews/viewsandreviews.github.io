Film Review

CODA

Capturing Oscars with Distinct Authenticity
Director Sian Heder
Year 2021
Starring Emilia Jones, Troy Kotsur, Marlee Matlin

CODA's an immediate crowd pleaser; a stress reliever.
A heart and at least 4 functioning senses.
Beaver with a fever.

As homeboy Jesus graces me with an itsy bitsy of time off from my NPC duties, I'll take advantage of the international egg hunting to catch up on these (Views &) Reviews. So here it is, the tepidly anticipated Oscar winner that came out of nowhere, CODA.

Definitely not my expected winner of the year, but a welcomed surprise nonetheless, CODA's biggest disappointment stays in its acronym standing for Child of Deaf Adults and not Cows OverDosing on Acid. Insulting my questionable humour is ableist against wit-deficiency, so back off.

✦

The lack of expectation in its winnings definitely didn't come from either a lack of quality, or a lack of Oscar bait. In fact, CODA does a great job at filling up not just its diversity Latino quota, but also its disabled quota in what cannot be described in any other way if not as a pro-gamer move. But all this said, CODA is still surprisingly distinguished from your usual best pic winners, and for this rare occasion I shall treat it as such.

Let's start with a brief introduction to its simple yet effective formula of a story. Snarky teen Ruby takes the lead in this family tragicomedy, with a family that fills up their lack of auditory abilities — exception being for young Ruby — with a bag full of both tragic and comedic timing. An outcast of sorts, Ruby has been taking a step back from her ironic dreams of singing for the masses to aid her fishermen deaf family in communicating with non-aquatic life forms. And if you can't guess how the story ends from its 15-minute lead-up, congratulations — you may have just earned yourself your own disabled card.

✦

Originality not counting, CODA does a bit of many things, all properly dosed. First and foremost, CODA answered my age-old question of whether deaf people love gangsta rap, or how a couple of deaf people sound like during passionate and soundproof coitus.

CODA unquestionably serves as last year's choice for best coming-of-age pic, a genre my anxiety-pumping heart loves oh so much, making for a proud and fine addition to their ranks. It also shines a light on the somewhat forgotten life of those who don't hear so good, as grandma would say.

✦

Simply put, CODA's an immediate crowd pleaser; a stress reliever. Beaver with a fever. And does as great a job at avoiding becoming an insufferable manifesto as my rhyming skills, which are unequivocally first rate.

And while it may not end up in most people's all-time lists, or even last year's top lists, for the least sensitive of you, it remains a more than satisfactory feels-good movie, with a heart and at least 4 functioning senses.
