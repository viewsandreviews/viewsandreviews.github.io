The Graduate / When Harry Met Sally

Two Rom-Coms That Break the Mold

Both succeed in capturing our need for support during our troubled times,
by means of human contact —
so underrated in these wireless modern times.

Ahh the rom-com. The go-to genre for the lazy, horny man looking for the good ol' in-and-out 30-minute date session, or the broken-hearted he/she looking to find more reasons to detest their exes.

These are nay but stereotypes, which not so surprisingly is also the most contradistinguishing attribute of all romantic comedies. Admittedly, I haven't been a huge aficionado of the genre. It's one of the more formulaic genres, which makes its purpose even more circumscribed in the previously mentioned clichéd lines.

But sometimes, there's that one movie — or in this case two — that slightly breaks the mold, or simply uses a higher quality apparatus mold, to provide more than a background distraction for the unimaginative single man.

A great example of such two movies are 1967's The Graduate and 1989's When Harry Met Sally. Indeed, a bit too boomerish to be candidates for your contemporary Netflix & Chill plans, but I'd still recommend both for anyone who's out to impress some old spirit, either in body or in soul.

✦

Let's start with The Graduate, the less stereotypical of the two and the one with qualities that go beyond those of the genre, making it a solid solo viewing.

One thing to get out of the way from the get-go and probably the movie's biggest misstep was trying to pretend that 30-year-old, recently divorced dad-of-two-looking-ass Dustin Hoffman could pass for a baby-faced newly minted graduate. Senior Hoffman is Benjamin, the oldest 21-year-old in the Western hemisphere.

Benjamin is shown from the very opening act as a doubtful and uncertain kid, questioning what to make of his new beginning in life, full of responsibilities and expectations. Things start taking a turn once the mother of all MILFs comes knocking at his door to help him get Hoff-man. And from there on out, the rest is whimsical, Simon & Garfunkel-infused history.

The end result is a classic and late-blooming coming-of-age story, very much focused on the coming aspect.

✦

Next, the movie that started the global man's paranoia of whether their motion in the ocean was actually keeping the boat afloat.

When Harry Met Sally — a much more standard recipe for the comedy of romance, albeit with a standard that has rarely been reached by competition. Undoubtedly, the main factor in the success of the movie are the titillating performances by Meg Ryan and Billy Crystal. So titillating they inspired me to sprinkle "titillating" into my everyday dictionary.

The duo carries and balances out the show, focused on opposite approaches in dealing with dysfunctional relationships, where the non-functioning part is often protagonist of the story.

✦

The first movie ends with a bittersweet, slightly anxiety-inducing final act, which perfectly sums up the moments of clarity we have after rushing into a love affair without any precaution, followed by the soul-crushing angst of social and personal responsibility.

The second movie offers a much sweeter, diabetus finale, which leaves us hopeful for an eventual happy ending in our own romantic trials and tribulations.

The Graduate fixates on the laissez-faire, spontaneous attitude of our early twenties, mixed up with the dread of entering adulthood. The second puts aside the more personal dilemmas to focus on the challenges of sharing one's destiny with another, where the challenges are often caused by those very dilemmas.

✦

That said, both succeed in their own way in capturing our need for support during our troubled times, by means of human contact — so underrated in these wireless modern times. In an all-too familiar development, the duo of flicks suggests that the strongest remedy we can or will self-prescribe is romantic copulation; that sweet, tender feeling that forces cracking sounds out of our IKEA beds.

But to the more focused ones of you, I leave you with a warning. If you were in the early phases of a blossoming affaire, either of these movies may test the resilience of future amorous pursuance, by forcing an intimate dialogue on painfully delicate topics.

So keep that in mind, you naughty naughty corndog.
