Everything Everywhere All at Once

Excessively Elongated Acronym As Omen
Directors Daniel Kwan, Daniel Scheinert
Year 2022
Starring Michelle Yeoh, Ke Huy Quan, Jamie Lee Curtis

EEAAO is frenetic, engaging, confusing and exciting.
It's also a story about family, marriage, pride and perseverance.
A story about most of us.

Ladies and gents, I am ecstatic to announce to you all we may have found a pre-emptive winner for this year's Views and Reviews award for best pop movie: Excessively Elongated Acronym As Omen. And do not be worried my children, ain't no chance I'm ever gonna spell out this title anytime soon, so go ahead and waste your time nonetheless.

Here is the rare flick that comes out of nowhere to spark a joyous break from our very quadri-dimensional blockbuster season.

✦

EEAAO is one of those rare films that keeps true to its namesake, giving all that can be given, bringing it to all corners of our mind-space, in one, delightful go.

The duo of directors formed by Dan Kwan and Daniel Scheinert — who previously graced us with such gems as Daniel Radcliffe playing a gaseous improvised water-vehicle — comes back together for a second time to parade their exuberant manifesto of cinematic extravagance and inspiration.

✦

Not only that, but they give precious Michelle Yeoh one more chance to shine her star in what may be her most memorable performance yet. Starting as one of many of those Shaolin Tiger Moms that western cinema has slowly been more and more accustomed to, Yeoh's character, Evelyn Quan Wang, is filled with troubles and tribulations of all sorts — from an ever-on-the-brink marriage, to a failing business and an impossible-to-fix relationship with her taco-loving daughter. Evelyn has to fight against all her inner demons to complete a very predictable but satisfying circle of life.

✦

To avoid making EEAAO one of many other family dramas that are often remembered for as long as one award season, the duo of directors and screenwriters bring a slew of demons and saviors from all of the Rick & Mortied dimensions of EEAAO. Still, the true multiverse observed in EEAAO is the one of Cinema.

With a beautiful blend of what made so many cinematographic traditions so great, and a modicum of modern twist, no moment of EEAAO is marked with either dullness or exasperating excess. With the frenetic energy of K-cinema to symbolize Evelyn's inability to break off and enjoy what she made her own, to the absurd humour of J-cinema, all blended in a corn-dough typical of Hollywoodian clichés.

✦

EEAAO is frenetic, engaging, confusing and exciting. It's also a story about family, marriage, pride and perseverance. A story about most of us.

It is smart when it needs to, but always descends back to our realm whenever its trajectory points too high, capturing ever so touchingly the vulnerabilities of its everyday characters, but also their strengths.

Undoubtedly the best movie to come out so far in this post-Covid era, what EEAAO will eventually symbolize in the Olympus of cinema has yet to be defined. Yet surely it freed many of the Titans of taboos of mainstream cinema to finally wreak havoc in the stale offerings the evermore scared Hollywood producers allow us in these troubled times.
