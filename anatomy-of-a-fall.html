Film Review

Anatomy of a Fall

When You Don't Know Why It Happened, Make a Movie About It
Director Justine Triet
Year 2023
Starring Sandra Hüller, Swann Arlaud, Milo Machado-Graner

When you don't know how it happened, ask why it happened.
When you don't know why it happened,
make a movie about it.

It is with pain — baguettes, to be precise — in my heart and a year of delay, that I have to admit it: the French are carrying European cinema. And while carrying it, they are letting it fall, with style, to take the rhythm of natural dances that would make one Buzz Lightyear smile in delight.

Anatomy of a Fall is that film in a nutshell — a movie that dives into the "what ifs" and "maybes" of life's most chaotic moments, then refuses to tell you what's actually true.

✦

The setup is deceptively simple: a French husband, Samuel, falls to his death from the attic of the idyllic Alpine home he shares with his German wife, Sandra, and their visually impaired son, Daniel. What follows is less a straightforward investigation and more an emotional dissection of their relationship — told through flashbacks, testimonies, and a child's fragmented understanding of his parents' lives.

The brilliance of the film lies in how it toys with perspective. Sandra, played with devastating nuance by Sandra Hüller, becomes the central figure in the courtroom drama, but the film never lets you fully trust her. Is she a grieving widow, or is her pain masking something darker?

✦

The same ambiguity extends to Samuel, who — though absent for most of the story — looms over the narrative as a complex, troubled man. Through glimpses into their marriage, its highs and its suffocating lows, we are left to sift through layers of love, resentment, and dysfunction.

And then there's Daniel, the film's emotional anchor, caught between loyalty to his mother and the loss of his father. His disability adds another layer of complexity — he hears more than he sees, picking up on the subtle tensions and unspoken accusations that the adults would rather keep hidden. His testimony in court becomes a pivotal moment: a child's interpretation of events used to unravel adult complexities.

✦

The courtroom scenes are riveting not because of their drama but because of their humanity. Lawyers, witnesses, and evidence weave a web of truths, half-truths, and lies, leaving the audience to play judge and jury. Was it a tragic accident, or was Sandra responsible? Every revelation feels like it's pointing in one direction, only for the film to swerve and remind you that people are messy, motivations are unclear, and truth is elusive.

Through it all, Anatomy of a Fall never tells you what to believe. Instead, it invites you to pick a side — or not. As you piece together the story of Samuel's fall, you realize the real story isn't about what happened that day, but about the relationship that led them there. A relationship that was, in its own way, falling apart long before anyone hit the ground.

✦

And as we, accidentally, fall — we reflect on the path that led us there. A path we carved ourselves, sure, but always under the guidance of a watchful God of Chaos. That's the kind of energy this film runs on: messy, unpredictable, and unapologetically human.

It throws you into a world where love and resentment live side by side, and where every answer creates another question. A beauty that doesn't rely on the answers it gives — spoiler: there aren't any — but in how it forces you to sit with the uncomfortable truth that sometimes there are no answers.

It's chaos wrapped in quiet reflection. A film that leaves you wondering not just what happened, but what it says about the way we live, love, and sometimes stumble through life. And after watching it, you might find yourself holding onto the handrail a little tighter.
