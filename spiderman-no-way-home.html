Film Review

Spider-Man: No Way Home

Sometimes 3 Memes Are Better Than 1
Director Jon Watts
Year 2021
Starring Tom Holland, Zendaya, Benedict Cumberbatch, Tobey Maguire, Andrew Garfield

Fan-service is a completely viable narrating strategy,
as long as it's done for a medium that doesn't try
or has never been more than just numbed entertainment.

First things first. No, I'm not surrendering to the allure of fame to talk about the hot new thing in town. But I do understand that my latest posts may have seemed inaccessible, vocabulary-wise. And to those comments I respond: Get good scrub, and learn to read junior high English.

And now that I am done with my weekly dose of unwarranted verbal aggressiveness, let's get friendly, just like our friendly neighborhood spidermen. Badum tss.

I will be friendly enough to warn you about the presence of major spoilers in this post, although if you're actually using my blog as a point of reference for your movie-going, you deserve all of the spoilers.

✦

Second things second. Yes, I did watch each and every Marvel entry since 20-something. Their blockbuster machine works on even the most impervious, which I am not even close to be. And while I cannot for the love of someone else recall the plot of half of their one-shot money grabs, I'll admit I have been mildly entertained by a majority of them.

No Way Home was not the case, as it went above and beyond any of my popcorn-munching expectations, thanks in no small part to its clever use of nostalgia manipulation.

Indeed, Spiderman 3 — the second one — does bring back the full gang of spideys onto one screen. Moreover, it does it in the most fan-serviceable way possible: through the power of memes.

✦

No Way Home and many of its Hollywoodian colleagues make it clear that meme culture has climaxed to the point of maturity where it's interchangeable enough with mainstream culture. It seems in fact that meme-lords have superseded the role of screenwriters and script supervisors in many of the latest blockbusters, with varied levels of success.

The plot of the movie is nothing out of the ordinary, considering the standards of extravaganza of the Multiverse in which the movie takes place. Spidey #3, Tom Holland's spidey, wishes for white-washed genie Dr. Strange to turn back time to the good ol' days when Mr. Tony sang him to sleep. But now he's stressed out. As Strange tries to aid Sp.3 by enchanting the people of the world into forgetting his secret identity, a series of Lemony Snicket's Unfortunate Events arises.

This results in a Jumanji-esque (the good one) domino of consequences, which sees an almost full rogue's gallery of past villains appearing, fighting for some screen-time.

✦

Everything flows like the better half of the habitual Marvel recipe, with a good balance of pace, arachnid action and heavy sprinkles of meme-infused humour.

No Way Home manages to accomplish all a modern day popcorn flick needs to do and more, even going back in time to remedy past mistakes. It also proves that fan-service is a completely viable narrating strategy, as long as it's done for a medium that doesn't try or has never been more than just numbed entertainment (I'm looking at you, Matrix Resurrections).

Tobey Maguire's spidey is also canonically crowned as the best spidey, which reignites a little sparkle of joy in the withering flames of our millennial hearts.

✦

Every character in the spiderverse gets to bite a little bit of the fan-service cake, with the cake being spread across two and a half hours of icy frosting. The run-time did weigh a bit on my Christmas-puffed shoulders, although all them spider-webs made for a smooth landing.

The story closes with some (un)expected heartfelt emotional baggage for viewers of all gens, albeit, again, its execution was as original as the McRib.

So yes, you may argue that Spider-Man: No Way Home is just an overly long recycled recipe of Marvel's more successful ingredients, but if Greta Thunberg ever taught me anything, it's that Recycling. Is. Good.
