Year in Review

NYE Wrap-Up

Pearl · Triangle of Sadness · Glass Onion

A seal of obligations shunning the shores of creation.
A mind that drifts and gives no shits.
Okay, let's get this going.

Well ho ho ho! My dudes and non-dudes. And no, I'm not coming to you with the latest featuring of Cardi B and Nicki Minaj. Instead, here comes the review Santa with a triple dose of Christmas spirit. A triple featuring of films, which with Christmas only share my decadent motivation of continuing feature-length reviews.

As motivation is the key critique; a critique of self-reflection, based on self-assessment of a shelved passion. A seal of obligations shunning the shores of creation. A mind that drifts and gives no shits.

Okay, let's get this going.

✦

Numero uno: Pearl

And what a cute little Pearl of quirkiness and rotten corpses this was, with future genre legend Ti West continuing his streak of success.

The rare prequel in the horror genre, and an even rarer quality prequel that arguably surpasses its predecessor thanks to the browless but emotion-full performance by Mia Goth. The Goth by name and looks gifts a mesmerizing Pearl, whose ambitions and desires mimic absolutely nothing one should ever feel empathic to — and if you do, shut down your TikTok account and get some help.

✦

Dos: Triangle of Sadness

Nordic power! Dare I shout, continuing Ruben Østlund's subversive satire of our political mundo. A satire that is more entertaining than clever, as it cleverly avoids any deep drilling in what is by now the farce of politics, and instead billiards its way through the other P's of passion. Pretty people, power, and puke.

An ensemble of stars, to-be stars and would-have-been stars (rip Charlbi Dean) is guided by the all-so-white hands of Mr. Østlund. An avant-garde film that steps back, as those last steps are a gift to the public instead of a feast of ego. In truth, it could have done for more arrogance, but that is just mine coming out with the speed of those '70s puke pumps, which find themselves a spot in the recession of innovation.

✦

Tres: Glass Onion — A Knives Out Mystery

Keeping up with the poignant yet partisan political propaganda, here's another slightly unexpected sequel of quality. Albeit one that won't remain in the annals of fame and glory, as it is far too time-circumscribed in its timeline of madness. One that entertains, but not as much as the first try. One that keeps the suspense tall, at least for quite some time — again, not quite as high as the other one. One that suffers most when put next to its sibling. Still, one that does more than most movies this year have done, or that any of you have done.

One that brings back Daniel Craig as the worst Texan accent in town to charm the audiences and kick mysterious arses. And Mr. Bond does all of it, as the one thread keeping together what is expected to be a new series of Agatha Christie homages.

✦

And now that all is chanted and wrapped, I thank these yearly highlights for cheering up this aging teen. A teen undeserving of Santa's flawless feature films, as they are often framed with a lump of bitter coal. A flavor we may be deserving of, and that sometimes reminds us to be less of a critic and more of a supporter.

And with this, I wish you all less failure with the yearly resolutions, as those discounted gym memberships won't pay themselves back, fatties.
