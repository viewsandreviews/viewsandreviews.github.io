Film Review

The Whale

Call Me Ishmellyalater
Director Darren Aronofsky
Year 2022
Starring Brendan Fraser, Sadie Sink, Hong Chau

"Call me Ishmellyalater."
— Brendan Fraser to his ex-wife,
post SAG award.

You may think, very erroneously so, that yours truly is a man of cultivated literature taste. A man who extrapolates the divine from Nietzsche and the mortal from Jesus. But in truth, I am a man whose consumption of textual lines only happens if alternated with fights between bald men and jailbait psychics.

That is all to say that I may have read a grand total of 10 novels worthy of my own memories in these 30 years of hedonistic absurdism. One of these, as you may have guessed, was indeed the most notorious of White Dicks, second only to John Holmes — Moby, for friends.

✦

And with great disappointment, I am here to say that in no way did it help in better appreciating this modern Whale served by Aronofsky. A really tasty whale, don't mind me. In fact, it's a whale that needs no appetizers, and most of all, no instructions on fully appreciating it — a commodity that breaks some of the director's shticks.

That's because Aronofsky is known for confusing intensity with humanity, and suffering with double dildos.

✦

But Aronofsky takes a step back, while still providing a masterful and enchanting raw visual of the wretched life of Charlie the Fatty, played by the majestic mummy hunter, Brendan Fraser. What is to come is a story wrapped around a dozen or less of days, unlacing the years of pain that have marked the suicidal, gay, obese protagonist (he's still a white man so don't feel too bad for him, Gen Z).

But this whale is truly a one-man show, the show of the real-life feel-good story of Fraser, in the role that only he could have ever played. A role that overshadows, by 200 pounds or so, all the additional great performances of the supportive cast. The role of a man who sees the positive in all but himself. The plus in people and his size description on Grindr, probably.

✦

He starts and concludes as both the captain and the whale, as the quest for meaningfulness is one so human as it is universal. Dammit, I knew that 6th-grade forced reading would come in handy eventually.

And so, all my non-fatphobic friends, I invite you to go and taste this endangered species of independent cinema, and mythical comeback story.
