Film Review

X

Granny Gonna Give It to Ya
Director Ti West
Year 2022
Starring Mia Goth, Jenna Ortega, Martin Henderson

X builds up on our age-old fear of horny grandmas,
just to make us realize there's nothing more horrific
than wrinkly old people trying to copulate.

Now that the quick parentheses of dutiful award recipients has been concluded, it's time to go back to the basics, my basics.

The basics that were founded by those not too obscure yet obscene movies with an attitude. Those that more than often rely on a baggage of proud homage to the classics, while spicing up the game with a freaky dose of whammy. Those that signed themselves with a resonantly dumb signature title; hence X.

✦

X is that rare, too rare, horror which immediately screams for modern cult title acquisition — as rare these days as an unmolested child actor.

Ti West continues his career as a homage artist to the classics of '70s and '80s slasher horror and then some, with what can be comfortably described as their unfiltered evolution. From captivating social commentary, to creating a somewhat familiar yet so iconically distinguishable villain — X does much of what the habitués of the genre may be eager to point out in Leo DiCaprio-style at their screen as so recognizable, yet so unmatched.

✦

Much of X's plot follows a standard recipe, albeit with enough of a twist to get my nips to Batman & Robin levels of excitement. In similar cowboy fashion as predecessors of the likes of The Texas Chain Saw Massacre and The Hills Have Eyes, X sees a group of city dwellers venturing to the deep, corn-filled, tobacco-spitting south of the US of A.

While much of the exordium follows what is to be expected by Ti West's continued tribute campaign, the gears are shifted quick enough from the all-too-American cliché of metropolitan vs. rural combat, to take an exciting and hilarious turn into unknown and unwanted territories.

✦

Instead, X builds up on our age-old fear of horny grandmas, just to make us realize there's nothing more horrific than wrinkly old people trying to copulate. A love story at heart, with other organs taking the juxtaposed meta-form of youthful ignorance and bliss versus old, nauseating regret. One of the many fortunes of X is in its avoidance of the overdone campy humour, which is instead substituted by an oxymoron of grounded yet over-the-tip-top montages.

Possibly still a step below the Olympus of 21st century movie horror, X still does the herculean feat of becoming one of their timeless demi-gods, continuing Ti West's upward parable of vintage horror success.

Signed, Xxx.
