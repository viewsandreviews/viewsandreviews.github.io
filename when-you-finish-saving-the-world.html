When You Finish Saving the World

Clean Your Room?
Director Jesse Eisenberg
Year 2022
Starring Julianne Moore, Finn Wolfhard, Alisha Boe

How many Gen Z-ers does it take
to turn on a light bulb?
Probably the same amount of sour millennials that it takes to write an original coming-of-age family drama.

How many Gen Z-ers does it take to turn on a light bulb? Probably the same amount of sour millennials that it takes to write an original coming-of-age family drama. Also, light bulbs are so old century, it's all about them red neon lights baby.

I digress. And to get back to the question, it's one. One sour millennial. Sour from years of well-deserved mockery, a recently screen-shy Jesse Eisenberg is here to hide his beautiful jew-fro behind a camera. Here to remind us of the frivolousness of activism, the ineptitude of the dreamers, and ultimately the prevalence of selfishness. Well, maybe that's not the message, but it seems to be a common misinterpretation, in view of the lukewarm reception of his directorial debut.

So let me make a stand as a surprised defender of the Berg. Cause his story is as captivating as it is needed, and more importantly, as it's entertaining.

✦

Starting with the provocative, albeit insufferable title. A provocation that is not just eye-catching, but that is dragged along throughout the whole story, lead by the improbable duo of Julianne Moore and Finn Wolfhard. Also, props to Finn to have never thought about using a stage name. Spanning a long generation of aroused Canadian werewolves, the Wolfhard continue to make a name for themselves.

Even more improbable is the accomplishment in the characterization of Evelyn, played by Moore, as her first and only detestable character. Mother to Finn's Ziggy, she's a woman whose passion for all has faltered long before, only kept alive by an ego that is all too relatable.

Ziggy is on his part just as obnoxious, but with more of a caveat. A self-described influencer of sorts, he slowly starts a journey of personal growth, moved by his gonads (mostly), and a little bit of rediscovered heart.

✦

Eisenberg is definitely in his element and does what he does best. Create a slew of characters as unlikable as his Lex Luthor performance. But these, unlike his screeching interpretation, are as unlikeable as they are alive. An unflattering world of bubble-wrapped narcissists, whose utterance is rarely an inner reflection, but a cast judgment void of any accountability.

His characters find what they were longing for in caricatured visions of their lost dreams, before ultimately finding each other. In a world of parallels, Ziggy and mommy look for the dream that never had been. Ziggy finds it in a mini Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez in her teen years, while Evelyn in a motherloving Oedipus boyo. Yikes.

✦

It is not long after they are confronted with a harsh reality, they luckily have no trouble accepting. For they may be lost in their missions, but in no way clueless, or 2-dimensional cynics, as the more critical may have described them. They're in fact a step away from accepting themselves and each other. A step that they dare to take, like we hope many others will.

When You Finish Saving the World ultimately does a splendid job of fighting the current. Reminding us that answers cannot always be found by looking up at the skies. Sometimes they require us to put our heads down, as a sign of humility and forgiveness. And maybe then, we'll be ready to learn.
