Film Review

All Quiet on the Western Front

War Never Changes
Director Edward Berger
Year 2022
Starring Felix Kammerer, Albrecht Schuch, Daniel Brühl

War has no heroes,
only survivors —
beautifully painted with the opaque colors of war.

War, war never changes. Unlike remakes. And remakes of remakes. Or so I hoped, as I watched the third iteration of All Quiet on the Western Front. A movie coming straight from the Krautland, a land that of war knows a thing or two, on a worldwide scale.

And leave it to those sneaky Germans to try to make us sympathise with a gang of young and innocent soldiers, just moments away from celebrating an illusion of heroic victory — before their turn in front of the line came, to reveal that war has no heroes, only survivors.

✦

Survivors beautifully painted with the opaque colors of war. Gritty and dirty from the very first moment a gun is thrown in their hands. And this beautiful portrait of war is probably what characterizes All Quiet on the Western Front, as the story is truly nothing historians and non-alike would have ever heard. Even the message that echoes in perpetuity is not of the original kind. A message that is all too clear, and which is pursued with arduous stiffness.

But it's that type of unflexible stiffness that forces the mind to wonder. Wonder in dark places, darker than trenches. So dark that I could not help but ask myself where people shit in these trenches. But worry not, as this movie jollies in giving you the answer you wanted and needed.

✦

In all honesty, not much more is offered. As we are all too desensitized from not just war, but pain, it's now a hard challenge to connect with these visions of humanism. The humanity described in these scenes is more of the mythical kind — an ideological interpretation, which often strips authenticity to pursue an accolade of technical talent.

But who am I to judge? As for technical talent, there is plenty for such a low-budget blockbuster. And in so saying, I'll allow a slow clap for ze Germans, who kept alive the mythos of European cinema for one more year, as the old continent's talent slowly decays.
