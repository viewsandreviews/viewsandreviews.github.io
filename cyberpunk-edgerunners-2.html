Anime Review

Cyberpunk: Edgerunners 2

Night City Still Doesn't Care About You
Studio Trigger
Year 2026
Episodes 10

Night City doesn't build monuments to its dead.
It just finds new people
to grind through the machine.

There are second seasons, and then there are second seasons. The first kind continues a story. The second kind has to justify its own existence — carrying the weight of something beloved while carving out space to breathe on its own terms. Cyberpunk: Edgerunners 2 is firmly the second kind, and it knows it. The question isn't whether it lives up to the original — it doesn't, not exactly — but whether it manages to do something worth watching. It does.

✦

The biggest weakness of this second season stems from its title. Calling it Edgerunners 2 immediately invites comparison to a show that told a complete story — David's arc was a tragedy with a capital T, burning bright and burning out in a way that felt definitive. A sequel using the same name suggests a continuation of that specific emotional register, and Edgerunners 2 is not that. It is looser, more sprawling, more interested in the city itself than in any single character's meteoric fall.

That's not a failure — it's a different show wearing a familiar jacket. Once you adjust your expectations, something genuinely compelling emerges.

✦
✦

At its centre is Roman Carax, a young fixer-in-training whose wide green eyes and perpetual scanner device mark him as someone still trying to understand Night City rather than conquer it. He's a useful protagonist precisely because he's not exceptional — he's smart enough to survive, not brilliant enough to be untouchable. His arc is the closest the season gets to the original's coming-of-age energy, and it lands hardest in the final two episodes when the city finally starts teaching him lessons he can't unlearn.

Beside him is Talia Yang, whose blue cat-slit cyberware eye has become something of the season's visual signature. She's the competence to Roman's curiosity — cooler, more guarded, carrying damage she doesn't advertise. Their dynamic is the emotional spine of the show, and the writers are smart enough to let it stay complicated rather than resolving into something clean.

✦

Weak Kingsley is the season's most memorable creation — an ageing mercenary whose face is half-destroyed, his eyes replaced by enormous red cyborg rings that somehow make him look both monstrous and exhausted. He is what Night City does to people who survive too long. Every scene he's in carries a kind of ambient dread, not because he's unstable, but because he's so stable — he's made his peace with the city and what it cost him. That's scarier than any rage.

And then there is D, present mostly as a shadow — a single green eye glowing in the dark, voice heard before face revealed. D functions less as a character and more as a question the season keeps asking: in a city built on visibility, on chrome and neon and performance, what does it mean to be truly unseen? The answer, when it comes, reframes the entire back half of the season.

✦

Night City itself remains the most consistent thing across both seasons — restless, beautiful, indifferent. Trigger's animation is still extraordinary when it wants to be, particularly in the mid-season action sequences where the frame rate drops and the style gets aggressive and expressive in that very specific way that made the first season feel like nothing else on television. The city breathes. The corporate towers loom. People fall off them and the city doesn't even blink.

Where the season stumbles is in its middle stretch, episodes four through seven, where the plot idles while the writers try to establish too many threads simultaneously. The pacing sags in a way the first season never allowed itself to — partly because Edgerunners 1 was essentially one long sprint to an inevitable ending, and this season is trying to be something more episodic. The ambition is right; the execution sometimes isn't.

✦

But the gut punch is there. Night City always finds a way to remind you that it doesn't care about your arc, your crew, your love story, or your plans. Edgerunners 2 earns its ending — not by repeating what the first season did, but by arriving at the same truth through a different route. The city grinds. Some people get out. Most don't.

It's not the sequel anyone expected. It might be the sequel the story needed.
