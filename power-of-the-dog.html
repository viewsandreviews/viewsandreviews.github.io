Film Review

The Power of the Dog

An Austere Western, Wrapped in a Velvet of Overconfidence
Director Jane Campion
Year 2021
Starring Benedict Cumberbatch, Kirsten Dunst, Jesse Plemons

A good, competent movie that does everything
an award-celebrated movie should do.
But that is also its biggest fault.

As Oscar season slowly approaches and public interest is at an all-time low, it is time for yours truly to start his very much unneeded tour de force of Academy 2022 faves. With no standout piece coming around this year, I'll start by stepping into new Hollywood's Oscar favorite. Enter The Power of the Dog, the first western ever made where no man, woman or Mexican has been shot — or so I recall from my distracted first and sole viewing.

Rounding up at 12 juicy nominations, there's little chance of stopping Benedict Cumberbatch from getting his first golden statuette. Unstoppable is also Jane Campion, who orchestrated this finely written ploy at capturing as many golden boys as possible.

✦

TPOTD procedurally builds up on award season's favourite clicks, which I'll avoid listing as I try to keep my promise of being a better PC humanoid. It's also a somewhat slow, character-driven movie, something I could never fault a movie for being. Au contraire, 'tis the genre that composes most of my personal scoreboard, with A Separation still ranking highest from the latest century.

Undoubtedly a movie's movie, with lots of movie, deconstructed into a minimalist movie. A good, competent movie that does everything an award-celebrated movie should do. But that is also its biggest fault — at no times does TPOTD feel more than that: a movie.

✦

For however great Cumberbatch may be at recreating Campion's fantasy of frail masculine front, he never feels anything more than that: a character.

Instead, what are supposedly rugged cowboys at the beginnings of the twentieth century look more like ballerinas rolled around in dirt. Campion's undeniable talent ends up being halted by her forced recollection of a world that doesn't belong to her, as it so often happens. And by no means is this due to any imaginative shortcomings on her part — some things just need to be lived to be understood, or they cannot be exposed as more than a fantasy. So what was meant to be a sincere showing of the true feels hidden under moulds of masculinity ends up feeling more like a masquerade dance; entertaining and blissful, yet so unreal.

✦

But maybe my masculine self is being overly mean to this very movie. Maybe it's just the lack of falsely advertised powerful dogs that has me grinding my gears. Maybe I should stop writing reviews.

Naah, if a kiwi can direct a western that feels like everything but a western and get lauded for it, an overqualified depressed boy can absolutely continue his useless blog.
