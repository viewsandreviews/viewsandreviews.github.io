TV Series Review

The White Lotus

Season 3: Bloodstains on White Sheets, Metaphysical Hangovers Included
Creator Mike White
Year 2025
Network HBO

Here we are again — checking in to our semi-annual
feels-good-to-be-poor tradition of watching very rich people
fail upwards in beautiful locations.

Welcome to The White Lotus: Season 3 — now with more lotus than ever, and just as much white as legally allowed. This time, the all-inclusive luv-it-till-u-die vacation opens its gilded doors in Thailand: land of smiles, males, females, and everyone in between. Doors adorned with exotic fruits and faces, rituals commodified into room service, and gods repackaged into yoga mats.

Repackaged is also the guest list, as Mike White doesn't venture far from his previous showcase of modern bourgeois self-unawareness — ranging from the naïve innocents, soon to be compromised, to the Wall Street fiends looking for spiritual solace. Familiar faces, all-too-familiar vices.

✦

Returning is also the series crescendo of tension, but with a vastly more parabolic structure. Where the known build-up to an explosive finale was standard, we are now delivered a relatively tepid beginning — tepid mostly because we've been conditioned to expect a bloody reward at the end.

The guests, instead, will slowly be accustomed to their own reflections — whether through introspection or what you might call outerspection. Be grateful for your commodities, for the vices they allowed you, and leave no space for afterthoughts. That is the ultimate message Mike White leaves each season, if we can call it a message.

The sea is indifferent. The sun is too warm. The lighting too flattering for people who are morally dehydrated. No time for guilt.

✦

Colonial guilt. Parental guilt. Sexual guilt. The show marinates in it — not to resolve it, but to let it seep through every scene like coconut oil on a sunburn.

For that purpose, Thailand turns into more than just a backdrop. It's a silent accusation. Its beauty whispers, "You don't belong here." But the guests refuse to listen. They think paradise is their birthright. Only to realize — perhaps it is?

The glamorous Mr. White is not here to offer moral comfort. Instead, we are confirmed in all our childhood suspicions that money does, in fact, maketh good. Fucking real good.

✦

That is the non-closure we are left with. And while The White Lotus might not offer closure, it undoubtedly offers deaths. Human sacrifices to the lord of the small screen — us. And sacrifices we receive in great abundance, as the series of ankle-and-neck-breaking twists is now greater than ever, all nested into a single final episode that leaves no time to breathe, or bathroom breaks.

And so, with passports stamped in shame and souls slightly more wrinkled than their linen shirts, our guests exit the White Lotus — not redeemed, not punished, but simply… processed.

✦

That's the comfort of The White Lotus: it doesn't try to preach. It just lets its characters undress in front of ancient temples and call it a journey. It lets them chase meaning, only to end up sipping turmeric lattes beside their own irrelevance.

Season 3 doesn't ask, "Who died?" It asks, "Who lived?" And more importantly: "Why?" Because in the end, Mike White isn't interested in solving the murder — he's interested in the rot. And we, the viewers, keep coming back. Not for justice. Not for catharsis. But for bloodstains on white sheets and metaphysical hangovers.

Namaste, bitches. See you next season.
