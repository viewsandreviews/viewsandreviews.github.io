Film Review

Poor Things

Compromised Experiments
Director Yorgos Lanthimos
Year 2023
Starring Emma Stone, Mark Ruffalo, Willem Dafoe

We are but reflections of our surroundings.
We absorb the light that converges on us,
shadowed by the presence of a multiverse of ideas.

Compromised experiments. Sexy time machines. A true reflection of myself.

My favourite gyro savant of all time, Yorgos Lanthimos, returns with another highlight — as if anything but was to be expected from the eccentric Greek god. Favoured by me and Olympus alike, Lanthimos extends his winning streak with a comedy that is dark and bright, both in colours and spirit. Poor Things is his latest success, as well as his second reunion with the recently coronated, second-most-talented Emma in Hollywood.

✦

It is not just a comedy that Lanthimos brings, as he would never shortchange the audience. It is a fantasy world of his own, drawing from many inspirations. A science-fiction where the science is for laughs and the fiction for the heart. Just like his previous collaboration with Stone, Poor Things may not be of his authoring, but it is as much his own creation as Stone's character — known by the single name of Bella — is to her own creator.

Bella, the beautiful. A new-gen monster of Frankenstein, resumed by a mad doctor who, for a change, is more human than monster. Not to be fooled by the prosthetic appearance of Dafoe's Dr. Godwin Baxter — he is no foe. He will reveal himself to have human qualities beyond all those which make one a man at first glance. All of which he has been stripped of.

✦

But where her creator lacks in human appearance, the enchantress Bella makes of beauty the name of the game. A beauty that comes not only from aesthetics, but from an innocence that all others in the world are deprived of. A nostalgic feeling that we crave — until our own modern mould returns to rule upon us. Bella is subject to the desires of many others, in flesh and inner flesh. But guided by the principles of her godly father figure, she continues the adventure. An experiment of life from which she must find a purpose, no matter the amount of genital rubbing that may take.

This latest entry into Lanthimos's pantheon is certainly his brightest in humour — arguably brightest in a horizon that sets more than twenty years in the past. Both Stone and an egregiously moustachioed Mark Ruffalo carry the tone with a comedy that is as lively in pantomime as it is poignant to the mind.

✦

Lanthimos mirrors the mad doctor's persona by indulging in his own madness. Playing with colours as much as he plays with time. He makes of this borrowed story his own — a signed tribute to the politico-social commentary of present and past. A socialist and feminist manifesto that for once stands on its own premise, converting pedantic paternalism into harmonious creativity.

But even after all of the creative accolades it will certainly receive, Poor Things will remain a success story of modest storytelling — one that never makes a fool of the spectator. Instead, it reminds us of our simplicities. Sometimes it even simplifies our complexity.

✦

Eventually, we will all be judged by our own creation; become the experiments of our own experiment. And as we experiment, we experience. All those variables that were to be suppressed become the flavour of a new recipe for living.

Moral of the story: nothing is a mistake if you observe it long enough. Nothing is true to me as it is to you. Take everything and make it your own — and take my opinion with as much weight as a French hooker's parental bonds. And while doing so, try to fill that mouth with a smile.
