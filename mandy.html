Film Review

Mandy

Or How I Learned to Stop Worrying and Love the Cage
Director Panos Cosmatos
Year 2018
Starring Nicolas Cage, Andrea Riseborough, Linus Roache

"For vengeance is an emptiness and he that seeketh it wasterth himself."
— Jeffery Farnol, 1921
"But holy guacamole does it make for some sweet ultraviolence."
— Nabin Chowdhury, 2021

Mandy oh Mandy, what a pleasant surprise you were Mandy. Now, let me slowly unveil you for these perverts, Mandy.

Mandy is a story about love, a story about loss. Mandy is a story about vengeance. Whether it may be out of primal survival instincts or just moral sadism, movie vengeance, done right, feels good. But enough about my fetishes, let's get back to Mandy.

Mandy is more, much more, in the most simplistic of ways. In primis, it is the best representative of neon horror, beating all the other 30 candidates of the genre to a pulp, quite literally.

✦

Cosmatos returns to his debutorial origins to create from the ground up an immensely improved work standing on the same building blocks set by his previous work: Beyond the Black Rainbow.

So what was the magic ingredient that enabled his latest work to succeed where he previously couldn't? I guess more intellectually-inclined critics may attribute it to the more coherent but cryptic script, or the late Jóhann Jóhannsson's alluring soundtrack, or more likely the sublime drug-filled cinematography. But a true MMM (Meme Movie Master, that is, you plebs) will certainly know there is a sole explanation: a cocaine-fueled [citation needed], off-meds Nicolas motherloving Cage.

Cosmatos brilliantly understands that the success in a Cage-driven flick relies not in whether the Cage fits the movie, but whether the movie fits the Cage.

✦

Mandy's psychedelic and vibrant stills not only cured my colorblindness, but create the perfect settings for Cage to roam free and show his true forms.

Cage is cast as the lover, who is unjustifiably deprived of his love and is turned into a loser, only to later fulfill the prophecy and ascend into an axe-wielding demon slayer.

The dark and twisted world created by Cosmatos is eccentric enough to out-weird the Cage, fulfilling what most cannot, and allows us to fully empathize with his delirium and quest for retribution.

✦

What makes Mandy stand out against a sea of mediocre slasher movies is its juxtaposition of the over-the-top, Cage-fueled action sequences to the dreamesque, '70s nostalgic aesthetics, although the former is the clear winner in the battle for the spotlight. And while some may find the Doom-level absurdity of the action sequences to be off-putting, I say to these: Sorry boss, but there's only two men I trust. One of them's me. The other's not you.

That's it. That's all I can or want to tell you about Mandy. All that remains to discover about Mandy, which in all fairness is not much, I shall let you strip down on your own.
