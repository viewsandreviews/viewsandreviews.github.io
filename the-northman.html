Film Review

The Northman

A Jester's Wet Dream that Drowns in Its Own Complacency
Director Robert Eggers
Year 2022
Starring Alexander Skarsgård, Nicole Kidman, Anya Taylor-Joy

Everything seems to be as it should be —
a finely tuned score, beautifully captured frames of bleakness.
Still børing.

As a celebratory gift for Norway's (I'm too lazy to google) X-th birthday and liberation from those Danish dogs, here's a short but joyful review about a lovely fairy tale by Danish author and Zeus lookalike, Saxo Grammaticus.

Director Robert Eggers' fascination and fetish for old costumed folks culminates in the scream battle that is The Northman. His Northman pays utmost respect to Norse mythos, with fidelity and gore galore, reminiscent of those late night National Geographic, historically redacted docos. As such, I shall respect Eggman's passion and compile this review in similar fashion.

✦

As most children's fairy-tales, The Northman recalls the story of a tiny child who must regain the throne left to him by his betrayed and decapitated father. He-who-I-forget-the-name ventures from ship to ship, from sheep village to rooster roundabout, sticking to a path of bare-knuckled violence, to reclaim what is his, by way of nepotism. Spraying masculinity out of all of its pores, the man of the North is covered by a barbaric Bordeaux tint of blood for most of his lifespan, and runtime. His favourite pastimes: a sportive session of land-Quidditch, with the occasional rapey dungeon break.

✦

Eggers brilliantly captures what we can only imagine old-timey jesters and storytellers' charm and proud confidence must have been in their fabled rehearsals. What he forgets doing, while being too busy waxing his hipster mustache, is adapting these stories for the modern man, or me. A man whose standards for attention go beyond sculpted and dirtied barbarians, to my misfortune. Eggers the type of guy to bring his own crafted cream made from fruitbat's milk to an artisanal coffee shop.

Robbie heavily relies on a mutual passion for Norse mythology by the viewer to render The Northman a truly immersive experience. And although, luckily for him, Norse weirdness by itself is enough of a gluttonous incentive, it is often not sufficient to keep ye from asking yourself what in Odin's name is wrong with these folks.

✦

Everyone in The Northman breathes as if it will be their last breath of uncontaminated air, screams of passion with the might of a giant capybara, and whispers in reflection with the sobriety of a three-legged mountain cougar.

And despite — well, because of — all of this, he still manages to make a movie about brutal vikings, with a spoonful of ghouls and shiet, børing. Everything seems to be as it should be, a finely tuned score, beautifully captured frames of bleakness alternated to bold and ardent scenes of battle; still børing.

This movie is a boring movie, not in its entirety, but for enough of its run to make you forget about all of the aforementioned qualities. And such is my disappointment, that I will have to backtrack from my initial promise of leaving you with a joyful review, and instead blue-ball your needs for jovial anticipation with my mighty hammer of disappointment.

Neste!
