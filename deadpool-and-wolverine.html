Film Review

Deadpool & Wolverine

The D in RDJ Stands for Double-Dicked by Disney
Director Shawn Levy
Year 2024
Starring Ryan Reynolds, Hugh Jackman

Disneyfication: noun.
The process of defecating on a once successful franchise,
by the one and only Michael the Rat.

The Merc with a mouth is back, and he brings along one of the sole survivors of 20th Century Fox's burnt-out house of comic cards: the Wolverine. Deadpool's 3rd titular appearance comes de facto with many on-screen surprises and nerd-erecting re-appearances. But no amount of A or B-tier cameos can hide the painful reality.

Superheroes are a dying breed, figuratively and literally, on and off-screen. The first round of superhero fatigue, started in the years of past, has not stopped over-encumbering us.

✦

Not to lose all hope. D & W is still a salvageable movie — salvaged mainly by the W in its title. Ryan Reynolds' Deadpool comes back with jabs for everyone. It's a reference fiesta, with punchlines coming in after every punch. Whether the jokes are directed at itself or at the audience, DP got plenty. And that's where sarcasm-fatigue comes in.

Unfortunately for the Reynolds, public banter had become the one universal qualifier of half the Marvel Universe, which put a big dent in Deadpool's reputation and role as bearer of the overdone trope. What hurts even more is the timing of said jokes. In a very expected twist, juggling the business of comedy with a compelling plotline is Cirque du Soleil level of difficulty. And the Pool struggles a few too many times to hold the balls in place.

The Marvel formula seems to have also partly inverted itself, dedicating a considerable amount of time to servicing the most die-hard of fans with a number of obscure comic references that only ChatGPT can help understand. That's where most of the critique ends, although much of the most recent Marvel outing isn't much else.

✦

The story is yet another one that sees not just one world, but an infinity of them at peril. A sizeable part is set in the Void — a heavily modded, Fallout-esque world where all discarded past characters are left for recreational use and torture. That's where Deadpool and Wolverine are stranded, after altering the rules of time in an often non-sensical universe.

That's also where we start to appreciate the buddy comedy at its most, thanks greatly to the balance provided by an always in-shape Hugh Jackman. His sombre performance traces back to one of the last of Marvel's greats, Logan. Slowly softened by the Pool's shenanigans, it's from the second act that the movie picks up steam — by actually releasing the pressure of comedy.

✦

The dichotomy of the duo works its best once the purpose of Deadpool's on-screen annoyance is made clear. The end result is a shake-up of the formula that makes the film what Flashpoint should have been — without still making it into what we have wanted — all at the Sound(track) of a bipolar suburban kid who gets sent to his room for skipping choir practice.

And with this, one more breath is given to the dying genre and the dying art of cinema as a whole, while we wait for the inevitable projection of a 2-hour collection of Instagram reels. Coming soon, in IMAX and 4DX.
