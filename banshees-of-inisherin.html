Film Review

The Banshees of Inisherin

A Muffled Ode to Masculine Repression
Director Martin McDonagh
Year 2022
Starring Colin Farrell, Brendan Gleeson, Barry Keoghan

"What's worse than losing a friend?
Losing a hand — for there is no warmth
comparable to that of your fingers on your gland."

There it is, the summary of my most anticipated 2022 movie. Itssa wrap. Also, welcome to 2023 from your newly crowned best doctor in town. For now, I am a doctor in spirit and fact, which furthers my distance from you, plebs. But I'll cut the self-glorification short, as one chop of a finger, or five, is not far, as demonstrated by The Banshees of the forgotten Inisherin.

Chop-chops that could come from our sweetest dears, our mortal enemies, or ourselves; so often fluctuating between the spectrum of the first two personas. Now, let's cut the mysteries, and be direct, as direction is what is lacking in the tragic life of our protagonists.

✦

A familiar duo for director Martin McDonagh. Familiar for a good reason, as none has climbed higher in his few yet brilliant takes as a director, to which this banshee is a welcomed addition. Colin Farrell is back at his peak, accompanied by his favorite co-stars, left eyebrow and right eyebrow. As it was the case in In Bruges and Seven Psychopaths, McDonagh remains the king of the orchestra, as his and his only baton can tame those two monstrous brows full of emotion and liveliness.

Pádraic Súilleabháin is his character, a name that may force you to spit out rainbows of potatoes if pronounced correctly. A man of simple taste and simpler desires. An island man. An Irish island man, more so. With an Irish island slowly breaking apart, as the echoes of the civil war are heard from a distance in this of Inisherin.

✦

A war between past neighbors; past friends; past families. A past that changes so swiftly and is forgotten while one awaits for a meaningful future. An allegory to breaking the sole friendship of our dear Pádraic, nice to a fault. For his fault is no other, when his dearest of (two) friends decides to break apart their friendship.

That is where the opposite lead to Mr. brow comes into action; the man looking for a future. Brendan Gleeson is that man. A folk man, who makes of music his nurture. A serious man, anchored by the sole facial expression Gleeson is capable of, but of which capabilities are a necessity for greatness.

✦

Banshee is a movie about conflict; about the often unexplainable nature of our conflicts and the 101 ways to deal with it. But it's also a movie about our dependency to conflict. As with conflict it still ends, with an ending that allows our duo of jesters to continue in their troubled, vindictive adventures. Till death sets them apart. Lovely.

✦

McDonagh is at his most somber, a somewhat natural progression from his Three Billboards. So much so that only his signature touches of political humor distinguish it from fellow Farrell puppeteer, Yorgos Lanthimos. But it's a somberness that won't leave you hungry, at least not for long. As its resolved and unresolved conflicts will linger for quite a while, as you are forced to rewatch the Banshees for the 11th time, in your attempt to understand those Irish noises without subtitles.

Lovely.
