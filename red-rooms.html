Film Review

Red Rooms

Arguably the Creepiest Movie Ever Made That Never Once Attempts a Scare
Director Pascal Plante
Year 2023
Starring Juliette Gariépy, Laurie Babin

What's scarier than a French murderer?
A French Canadian murderer.
Or maybe just a French Canadian.

Pascal Plante welcomes you into his Red Rooms — arguably the creepiest movie ever made — to never once attempt a scare.

The film starts at a very poised pace, a kind synonym for snail-coitus-level action. But what takes time to ingrain itself is often rewarded with inescapable suspense. We jump immediately into a trial for murder. Three young teens. One creepy man.

What used to be the recipe for my nights out of a time long lost is now the background for these so-called Red Rooms: rooms where the murderer streamed his abuse, torture, and execution of these teens, to accommodate the desires of an invisible, perverted audience.

✦

At the trial's attendance, we are introduced to our protagonist. Kelly-Anne (Juliette Gariépy) is a figure of eerie composure — a successful model whose life seems sculpted into perfection, yet whose nights are consumed by livestreams of a sadistic trial. Her fascination with the accused man is never explained, nor judged by the film — it simply is. And that refusal to grant us easy answers is what makes her so terrifying.

Her morbid curiosity reflects our own, dragged by Plante's manifesto of demand and request. Watching her, you're struck less by her vulnerability than by her resolve — the way she drifts deeper into the darkness with the calm of someone grocery shopping. She isn't swept away by events; she volunteers to drown in them.

✦

The film cleverly uses Clémentine (Laurie Babin), a neurotic fangirl of the man under trial, as Kelly-Anne's chaotic foil — and to regulate the tempo of an otherwise unassuming first act. Clémentine is jittery, excitable, and morbidly curious. Our proxy, in many ways.

As her character recedes from the story, the spotlight returns to Kelly-Anne, and that's when the film sharpens into something truly unnerving. With Clémentine gone, there's no one left to break the stillness, no one to translate Kelly-Anne's silence into something we can digest. We're forced to sit with her void. And it's crushing.

✦

There will be no shadows leaping at the camera, no killers with knives, no shocking jolts in the dark. Instead, we are left to endure the horror that resides in the way Kelly-Anne's obsession incubates around us — in the droning weight of an increasingly suffocating soundtrack, and in Gariépy's performance: a masterclass in restraint that makes every glance and pause feel like a knife slowly being twisted.

The monster isn't in the courtroom, or even online in the so-called red rooms. It's right there on Kelly-Anne's face. Unreadable and unrelenting.

By the end, you don't leave with answers. You leave with unease — a recognition that the most disturbing minds aren't always the ones on trial. Sometimes they're the ones taking notes from the back row, watching, waiting, perfectly still.

We leave confused, consumed, and still slightly frightened.

Truly, a French-Canadian experience.
