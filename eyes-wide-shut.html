The movie that broke the Kubrick spell at 14 — and the one that only made sense years later, after the illusion of non-consequential hedonism had broken for real.
Film Review

Eyes Wide Shut

The Christmas Movie That Killed Christmas
Director Stanley Kubrick
Year 1999
Starring Tom Cruise, Nicole Kidman, Sydney Pollack

That is what Eyes Wide Shut was trying to warn me about.
It just took living it to finally understand.

It was 14 years ago. The day when it began.

I was in front of my dusty-as-ever computer monitor, having recently discovered the beautiful world of "alternatively legal" movie streaming. My days were voraciously consumed by my need for entertainment, which now came in the form of slasher horror movies. It didn't take long before I exhausted all of the gory flicks found in the limited online libraries of the time.

I needed more; I wanted more, although what I wanted was not so clear to me anymore. I realized filling my belly with a strict diet of horrendous B movies started to cause quite the stomach ache. I needed a prescription, and cowbell was not it.

✦

There it began; the start of my journey into the world of movie discussions and reviews. While I was looking for a remedy to my incessant hunger, a name kept echoing in the back of all the forums I consulted to find answers: Stanley "the Man-ley" Kubrick.

And Stanley was definitely the man for me. From the daunting sound of a tricycle echoing in the empty corridors of the Overlook Hotel, to the gang of milk-empowered droogs at the Korova milk bar; there was nothing Kubrick did that did not resonate with my juvenile brain. Yes, I know it's cliché. A little teenage boy enamoured with the king of pseudo-intellectual 14-year-olds. Look at the bright side, at least it wasn't Tarantino.

But then it came, the movie that broke the spell. The one where my beloved Kubrick stopped resonating with me: Eyes Wide Shut. My little 14-year-old mind was wondering: what is missing? What am I missing?

✦

Kubrick did touch up on shared themes of obsession with excess, with the obscene and with the taboo in two previous favourites of mine: A Clockwork Orange and Lolita. But this time I felt estranged by the Christmas-y New York of the movie and by the troubles of its protagonists, doctor Bill Harford (Tom Cruise) and wife Alice (Nicole Kidman).

The movie follows the well-off (and then real-life) couple enjoying the prestigious life of the Big Apple's upper class. An argument breaks out between the Miss and Mister, after a Christmas party where they reciprocally and inadvertently end in the claws of other lustful attendees.

Alice, after noticing her husband being attended by two not-so-subtly libidinous models, later confronts him in the privacy of their NYC apartment. Billy comforts her, stating that those lovely ladies of the night were not after his M.D.(ick), because wahmen ain't horny like that. Alice responds with maniacal laughter. She then goes on to confess that this propped-up life they have been living has brought her to fantasize about abandoning it all for some good ol' sailor wood.

Billy's cluelessness about the necessities of his partner and his understanding of desire was not implausible. In fact, I was just as clueless.

✦

Bill Harford started wandering the holiday-themed streets of New York, looking for answers. And during an in-habitual calm stretch for the usually rushing Tom Cruise, his character ends up intertwined with the world of masqueraded, lustful opulence.

Eyes Wide Shut unveils the not-so-secret hierarchy of the most gluttonous, most lustful, most greedy. Those who arrange themselves in pyramidal manner based on their escalating lack of remorse and empathy.

It was a puzzling dream that I was seeing on my monitor. A world too unfamiliar to me to be able to connect to Kubrick's criticisms. But that was soon to change.

✦

You see, it was not too long after that I started frequenting the snobbier crowd of monocle aficionados of my then hometown. I was warmly welcomed into their dwellings, to my initial incredulity. It was not so later that I found the catch. I was treasured by my new squad of groupies as their personal fetish. A fruit that had been forbidden to them, which made it all the more appetizing to savour.

The secrecy of it all added a layer of excitement and enticement to them, just as much as it did to me. I was not much different than one of the harlots shown at the Caligula-styled parties of the movie, unidirectional power dynamics and human sacrifice notwithstanding. It didn't take too long for the illusion of non-consequential hedonism to break. When you are at the bottom, the consequences of your consumes are both unknown and severe; something my concubines knew all too well, and the thrill of danger made it all the more enticing to them to continue the pursuit.

That is what Eyes Wide Shut was trying to warn me about.

✦

The Christmas setting of the movie adds an additional veil of criticism of the ever more consumeristic society of the late '90s. A society where the desire to consume supersedes any feeling of compassion. A society where shame is replaced by the thrills of covert overindulgence. Just like a big, beautiful Christmas gift we gift ourselves and proudly photograph, tidily wrapped; knowing all too well the indecent content we captured, and reveling in the publicity of its naughty naughty secrecy.

One last applause goes to the brilliantly haunting score by Jocelyn Pook, which was much of the reason I revisited this anti-Christmas classic, and re-connected to the last remaining piece of my Kubrick puzzle.

Now go fill up your Christmas bellies with all the extravaganza you need, but remember, when it comes time to purge, don't do it beneath the Christmas tree. Because that's not the Christmas spirit.
