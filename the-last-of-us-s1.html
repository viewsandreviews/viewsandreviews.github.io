TV Review

The Last of Us

Laude Subverted Innovation
Creator Craig Mazin, Neil Druckmann
Year 2023
Starring Pedro Pascal, Bella Ramsey, Anna Torv

Adios Resident Evil.
Step aside, Lara Croft.
Sayonara world of mediocre game-based adaptations.

Adios Resident Evil. Step aside, Lara Croft. Sayonara world of mediocre game-based adaptations. Welcome to the new world. The world of Pedro Pascal, conqueror of digital media, and tiny sidekicks. Shoutout to my man Luigi, the first and only sidekick of greater size and even greater sex appeal than his counterpart. Still humble in his endeavor.

✦

More importantly, welcome to the world of Last of Us; a world I've been excited to visit for a decade since the first rumors of PC ports by those greedy Playstation hoarders. A world I have postponed reviewing in fear of ill-fated finales; a much worse reality than a bad shroom trip. A world of crumbles, perfectly built up by the fateful set designers, aided by a full production team who learned that the best way is the old way — until season 2 kicks in. A world that remains co-protagonist, as was the case in the gorgeous video game experience.

And what a world! Screams my reproductive organs, obscenely excited by the sight of my guiltiest of pleasures: post-apocalyptic desolate lands filled by patches of human resistance.

✦

Human perseverance is what is provided by the valiant duo of protagonists, Pedro Pascal and Bella Ramsey. Both forego pre-running critique susceptibility and give a performance for the ages, with particular praise for the more doubted and new to-go snarky teenager, Bella.

But let's avoid fast forward and start from scratch, as we are delighted to scratch the bearded Joel, voted most desirable bachelor of the Apocalypse. Pedro's Joel is a man with a past, some of which we are made aware of from the very start; some more that is left locked in an open dark room. A survivor from the old world, who does things the old-fashioned way. The American way. Bang bang. A man who survives to forget what he lived for, until he finds a reason to wake up another day.

Here comes Bella Ramsey's Ellie. A young teenage jokester from the new world. An amalgam of all the characters played by Ellen Elliot Page, with a dystopian twist. Ellie's special. Special enough to reignite the bits of humanity left within Joel, and possibly of the whole of humanity. Unfamiliar with the old ways, Joel will have to Virgil her through a mission of survival and of growth. A self-purposed journey through a world full of zombiesque enemies, that would make even the master of zombies Romero shed a prideful tear.

✦

A classic story of tragedy, survival, parental relationships, and humanity; all of the classic tropes are latched together in a tight, audience-friendly package, while still provocative enough to make you squirm with discomfort. All the beautiful memories are ported from their original inspiration, albeit in a format of uncommon brevity, and with little addition of original significance.

It may not win any accolade of originality, but that said it is its own accolade, as it reaches legendary status by simply being one of two great video game adaptations, second only to the masterpiece known as Angry Birds.

✦

Still, it does everything that is supposed to do. And it does it so well that we are left to wonder: what has incapacitated all of the previous attempts to bring game-based stories to the big and small screen, without reducing them to porn-parody quality? Shame on you I say, shame on you.
