TV Review

Lanterns

In Brightest Day
Network HBO / Max
Year 2026
Starring Kyle Chandler, Aaron Pierre

In brightest day, in blackest night,
No superhero action in sight,
Let those who worship CGI beware —
Great television is in the air.

It was, admittedly, not so long ago that I had completed the first (and only) season of what is unarguably the best television series of all time, True Detective.

It has been since that time and place that I have been longing to replicate the ambush of thrills and emotions the buddy detective drama had sparked. Many were the attempts at that. None succeeded. Not even Pizzolatto himself managed to ever recreate that impossibly ambitious formula.

Which makes the realization that a story about two intergalactic peacekeepers with magic rings is the closest link to that ever more mind-blowing.

✦

(Green) Lanterns — the officious title they go by. Conjuring anything the human imagination can produce is their trait. The entire cosmos is their jurisdiction. Space. Time. The fundamental physics of willpower itself.

They spend most of the season in Rushville, Nebraska.

Population: not many. Conspiracy: considerably larger than the zip code suggests.

And not a single shot fired from outer space in the first four episodes, which for a franchise that once gave us a CGI Ryan Reynolds suit is, frankly, the most radical creative decision of the decade.

It works. Magnificently.

✦

The murder mystery is the vehicle. The real journey is two men — Hal Jordan and John Stewart — who would not, under any normal circumstances, choose to spend time with each other, slowly realizing that the other person might be the most interesting problem they have ever encountered.

Kyle Chandler's Hal is an artistically refined Frodo Baggins. Once a chosen one, representative of the highest qualities a man could wish for, plus a very fallible but irresistible charisma and humour. He ultimately falls victim to the power, and mostly, the responsibilities that come with carrying such a burden.

Aaron Pierre's John Stewart is his philosophical opposite and his narrative equal. Marine. Architect. Chosen directly by the Guardians of the Universe in a break from ten thousand years of protocol, which is the cosmic equivalent of getting a letter of recommendation from Mr. Archangel Michael. Pierre carries the character's layered history without ever letting the weight show on the surface. It shows underneath.

✦

The restraint in action sequences — which one suspects is partly budgetary and entirely the right call — creates the space for the show to breathe in ways that the genre almost never allows itself. When something extraordinary does happen visually, it lands harder precisely because the show hasn't been spending your attention all episode.

Lanterns understands that spectacle is a currency and that the best way to make it valuable is to spend it rarely.

But we cannot start the analogy with the goat, True Detective, without answering the question of where the show lands in comparison. The answer is clear already in the initial episodes. The plot creaks, as imaginable, in places where it has to service the wider DCU machinery rather than its own story. There are moments where Rushville, Nebraska has to make way for the Green Lantern mythos, and they are the moments the show is least itself.

The symbolism is noticeably adapted from that of a comic book created almost 90 years ago. The mythos of the lanterns cannot withstand the mysticism in Pizzolatto's one-season wonder. None of the villains stand a chance against the eerie aura the Man with the Scars had surrounding himself, even before his on-screen appearance.

✦

But comparison is the thief of rings.

And even though the finale will not leave you with the same sense of emptiness — since the inevitable continuation has obviously been already announced — it will luckily leave you longing for more superhero deliciousness of the artsy kind.

What Lanterns ultimately gave us is a show that is more interested in its people than its powers.

Rushville, Nebraska. Population: even smaller than 5 minutes ago.

Stories worth telling: considerably larger than expected.
