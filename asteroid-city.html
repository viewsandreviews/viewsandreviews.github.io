Film Review

Asteroid City

Life After Wes
Director Wes Anderson
Year 2023
Starring Jason Schwartzman, Scarlett Johansson, Tom Hanks

It's all there, it's all good,
and it's all Wes.
The City of Wes.

Asteroid City. A movie by Wes Anderson. A funny movie. Do you know what's funnier? All of the enraged movie critics; enraged at the always arrogant Mr. Wes Anderson, making a movie that can only be described as a very, very Wes Anderson movie.

A sad extrospection to start with this time. Unnecessary and unjustified you could even say. But that is the times. I truly hate aging. That may be why I will try to combat the homogeneity of the aged mind with a hot take. Well, a tepid take, possibly.

Asteroid City is Wes Anderson at his near-finest. The vibrancy of the colours; the hyper-petulant dialogues that could make a narcissistic schizophrenic run in fear; the meticulous spider-web of plots, finely tuned to suspend and capture. It's all there, it's all good, and it's all Wes.

✦

Now that all that needed to be said has been said, let's start again with the superfluous — the plot. Two main stories are alternated: a colorless narration and in-production BTS of a play, and the colorful play itself, Asteroid City. That's also where we're immediately transported to, a Willie Coyote-inspired desert town, populated by 1, visited by hundreds.

The beautiful set is soon filled with all the stars and guest stars of the show, as a number of subplots start to unravel. But let's focus on the main story, or the main play — a tale of uncertainty.

Golden boy Jason Schwartzman is Augie Steenbeck, a very recently widowed father of 3. He has transfused his intellectual skepticism to all of his offsprings, while being surpassed by his eldest genius son. That's also the motive of their journey, as a conference for brilliant young stargazers is being held in Asteroid City. Why Asteroid City of all places? Because it's a city of science. A city of discoveries. But more importantly, a city of asteroids — falling stars.

✦

Here's also where the protagonists meet the pre-super Nova star, Midge Campbell (Scarlett Johansson). This worldwide known actress also accompanies her daughter to be honored at the convention. That's when we discover that Asteroid City is also the city of love, as love is what ensues between the two couples of parents and children. Don't mix it up.

Now, before you are fooled into thinking that will be a straight-forward plot, think again; as all straight-forwardness is lost faster than at a Birkenstock convention. We are soon introduced to all the other convention participants, ranging from starred generals to black cowboys. Oh, did I mention the alien? Yeah, Jeff Goldblum is in here too.

That's when chaos ensues. Because chaos is now the protagonist. Uncertainty as I had first defined it. Uncertainty as life proceeds without a long-life partner. Uncertainty of a life that has peaked, now in a deterministic twilight.

✦

Yes, I know. Been there, done that. Especially by the Wes. Much of his career has indeed been an exploration of the world of uncertainty — that's what Bill Murray has been here for all these years. And as Murray ages and cannot captain Anderson's stories no more, the storyteller matures, and his story ripens.

That's the new Wes. Much like the old one, but more of himself. A lot more, as he brings forth his usual crew of cavaliers and then some. Honestly, haven't seen a movie so crammed with stars since my Blacked subscription expired.

Life in Norway hasn't been so bad, after all.

✦

But the hoarder of talent and story never forgets to organize his pasticcio meticulously. Whether it be by direct exposition, or by re-aligning juxtaposed horizons.

What I can critique of this newly-old Wes is his insistence on relying on rigid performances; alienating, and not in the Jeff Goldblum-y kind of way. This is especially true when the task of following all of the petulant on-screen chit-chatter is already enough of a headache. But that's it; my one and almost only criticism of Asteroid City.

The City of Wes.
