TV Review

Fallout — Season 1

In & Out of Love with You
Creator Jonathan Nolan
Year 2024
Starring Ella Purnell, Walton Goggins, Aaron Moten

The man. The worker.
The legend.

War never changes. Videogame screen adaptations do.

Following the success of The Last of Us, the often overshadowed J. Nolan comes back with a first season up to his usual standards. The second new world created by the sometimes acclaimed screenwriter and director goes by the name of Fallout — another sometimes acclaimed game series that, just like him, has recently suffered heavy blows of criticism.

But be afraid not. Nolan captures perfectly the dystopian future satire and atmosphere that made the games a fan favourite. Indeed, a big-budgeted fan series is what we get. And that's also what we wanted.

✦

Here we are in the newfound dystopia, where nuclear conflicts between a very real US and China created the perfect precondition to a Mad Max world of madness and half-budgeted costumes. This is a story Fallout 3 fans may feel familiar with. Or Fallout 4 fans. Story writing was never their forte.

The naive protagonist. The overzealous loser. The bad boy with a soft past.

A human-sized Vault-Tec bobblehead is the main character we follow around, alternating to the view of her future companions to get a broad glimpse at a full universe. This bobblehead sure gives a +1 in cuteness, with possible side effects of naivety and lowered survival.

Lucy MacLean — the optimised result of generations of breeding in underground vaults, used for repopulation after the bombs fell. She is naive to the true nature of the cruel world she is born in, in and out of her Vault. A naivety that is often shared with all the others in this post-apocalypse. Just at different stages.

✦

Soon the chaos of the lawless world above slowly permeates into the infallible vault. That's when the heavy locked doors are made open and reality slowly breaks all her ideals, until it doesn't. That's also when she starts interacting with a new world and meets the other two thirds of the trio.

The Ghoul: a man of action. A man that steps to the sound of his own soundtrack. A fake cowboy of past and real gunslinger of now — Walton Goggins keeps his habit of outshining his co-stars alive, thanks partly to the writing that gifted him the more complex character out of an otherwise more than ordinary trio.

The rookie: a copy-and-paste The Force Awakens Finn, in looks and character. He joins a legion he has always looked up to, until the ideals for which he thought they stood for are revealed as anything but truth and justice.

✦

The intro is plain and simple. But those simplistic stories make for a perfect first introduction to the world of Fallout. The corruption of power — shown at different stages and speeds. The fight for survival, one that comes before morals and ethics.

As with many other what-ifs, the world of Fallout is one of extremes. Although what's parodied is a world of old or new, the metaphor focuses on the real present. One that is outside of nuclear waste, for the time being.

The guiding hand of Nolan is not so different from the tutorials we often have to spend our first minutes in-game. But this tutorial has no dullness — instead, it is a much-appreciated dumbing down. What would have been in-your-face moral lessons are now not as painstaking. The humour and silly world-building do a great job at covering the absurdity of science and reality at hand, similar to the "features" shared in-game.

✦

What doesn't work are the very cartoonish costumes and sets, which confuse about what the intentions of the screenwriters are. Any attempt at building gravitas is short-lived, as humour and splatter reign supreme. Those nightmarish moments of vault exploration — nerve-wracking in the game version — are lost to an adventure like many others. The darkness hidden within the walls shines with a bit too much of a brighter smile to evoke the right emotions.

That said, the overabundance of humour does help to highlight the seldom pearls of dialogue scattered between the episodes.

The last episode, the grand finale, almost ruins all the grand efforts created so far with a sudden jump-scare of Justice League quality special effects — scarier than any super mutant to come. We got a Crimson Chin live-action before GTA 6.

✦

But with just 8 episodes, Nolan and his companions are able to capture both attention and curiosity, for a story that will undoubtedly build up as the seasons go on — hopefully following an opposing pattern to the shark-jumping Westworld.
