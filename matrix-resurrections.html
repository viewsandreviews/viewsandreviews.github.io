Film Review

The Matrix Resurrections

As Master King Once Said: Sometimes Things Are Better Left Dead
Director Lana Wachowski
Year 2021
Starring Keanu Reeves, Carrie-Anne Moss, Yahya Abdul-Mateen II

The first true dud I'm reviewing.
To spare myself the suffering of multiple watches,
I'll keep this as short as my latest relationships.

I'd like to take a moment to thank the Wachowski -insert pronoun here- for stringing a thread of forgettable flicks after their miraculous (adopted) prodigy child: the Matrix. This made it all the more tolerable to accept this movie was going to be a dud.

And oh boy, was this a dud; the first true dud I'm reviewing. And to spare myself the suffering of having to go through multiple watches of this snoozefest, I'll keep this as short as my latest relationships.

I'd also like to preemptively excuse myself for the quality of this post, as frustration was the sole driving force behind it. And if rumors were to be true, it may very well have been the only driving force of this unneeded sequel, as the Big (Warner) Brother was said to have coerced its production.

✦

Let's start with the best bits about this resurrected and yet decomposing mummy. Yes, the movie does have its redeeming qualities, and I'm sure I would find even more if lady sleep didn't get the best of me during most of the screening.

Ironically, what is probably best about the movie is its self and broader criticism of the media industry, which is tackled with the implicity and tenderness of a wild boar in heat.

The roles are now reversed. What was the virtual world is now the reality the Wachowski so much despised. The real world (and what people remember as The Matrix) becomes the product of corporate greed, which director Lana pokes before ironically constraining herself in its rules.

✦

The movie finds a bit too much pleasure from self-referencing its sole, I repeat, sole predecessor, which happens not just in dialogue but also in the form of constant flashbacks.

That might also be the greatest mistake of ~~Matrix 4~~ Matrix 2; continuously reminding audiences of the existence of a much more pleasant time. A time when a partially original story of rage against the (identity) machine was combined with exciting, all new gun-fu styled action and sexy leather attires.

Now those same attires are forcefully imposed on barely walking middle-aged has-beens, who mostly stand there looking uncomfortable while reading out loud unbearably dull exposition dialogue.

Also Neil Patrick Harris is the main villain and there's no Hugo Weaving's Agent Smith. Yeah, F this. I'm out.

✦

Now go enjoy your New Year's festivities and get ready for one more year of soul and childhood-crushing sequels, remakes and reboots.
