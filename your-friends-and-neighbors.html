TV Review

Your Friends & Neighbors

Comfortable People Discovering That Comfort Was Mostly Decorative
Creator Jonathan Tropper
Network Apple TV+
Year 2025
Starring Jon Hamm

There was a time when television served a very specific demographic fantasy.
The global Corpowives-Triad: Manhattan cosmopolitans, suburban socialites,
and their spiritually identical cousins scattered across gated communities worldwide.
Their biggest existential crisis was choosing between a charming man
and a slightly richer charming man.

Fast forward twenty-five years, and the husbands those shows quietly revolved around are now staring down the barrel of AI agents that can do their PowerPoint presentations without the existential sighs. Thankfully, Jonathan Tropper's series arrives just in time to provide them the comfort they needed, in between golf drives and coke drive-bys.

✦

At first glance, Your Friends & Neighbors almost feels algorithmically generated, with a premise that writes itself: affluent suburbia, financial scandal, fragile marriages, suspicious neighbors, big criminal twists already visible from the first sipped martini.

For a moment, you begin to suspect Tropper's job might also have been quietly outsourced to a neural network trained on prestige-TV clichés.

And yet, somehow, the thing works.

The show knows exactly what it is: a glossy dissection of comfortable people discovering that comfort was mostly decorative.

✦

Jon Hamm leads the pack with the exact brand of weary charm he has perfected over the years — the man who still looks like he belongs in a luxury watch commercial, when in reality his watch may have been retrieved by less lawful means. Hamm leans into that contradiction beautifully, making his character feel both smug and strangely vulnerable.

Around him, the ensemble moves with the well-rehearsed rhythm of people who have spent years pretending not to notice each other's problems. None of the performances scream for attention. Everyone behaves as if the social contract of suburban respectability still matters, even as the ground beneath it slowly dissolves.

✦

Where the series stumbles is often in its dialogue. Tropper writes conversations that occasionally land with the elegance of someone explaining their feelings via corporate memo. Characters say the quiet part out loud with such theatrical performism you half expect them to turn to the camera and invoice you for emotional transparency.

It can be unpleasant to the ears — a bit too polished, a bit too rehearsed.

But visually, the show remains consistently delicious.

Your Friends & Neighbors understands that wealth is best filmed like a Discovery Channel after-dark documentary: wide lenses, immaculate interiors, sunlight bouncing off surfaces that cost more than your car. The camera lingers on perfectly curated spaces that feel increasingly hollow the longer you stare at them. The aesthetic is aspirational in the way luxury hotels are aspirational — beautiful, sterile, and vaguely unsettling once you remember nobody actually lives there.

✦

And maybe that's the series' quiet trick. Beneath the predictable scaffolding of suburban drama sits a mild but persistent critique — and its more than obvious rebuttal.

Success without purpose is just very well-decorated anxiety.

That said, anxiety is best experienced in the front seat of a Maserati GranTurismo.

That's why the series, and all of its metrosexual cousins, must deliver exactly what they promise: attractive people behaving badly in beautiful houses while pretending everything is fine.

And if that fantasy once belonged to our beloved Corpowives, perhaps today it belongs to everyone nervously refreshing their inbox, wondering if the next email will politely inform them that their Teams-refreshing skills are no longer needed.

Luckily, Claudebot is no fan of country clubs.
