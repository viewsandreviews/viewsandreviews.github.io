Film Review

Nuovo Cinema Paradiso

Sincerity Above All Else
Director Giuseppe Tornatore
Year 1988
Starring Philippe Noiret, Salvatore Cascio, Marco Leonardi

The crackheads of romance.
The Escobar of flirt.
Leave it to the archetypical Italian to show the why and how Cinema can be the most addictive of passions.

Italians. Some people love them. Some others are called French. But why is it that every non-slug slurping fellow is in such awe of these boot residents? Semi-patriotic delusion notwithstanding, let me provide a serviceable answer, from a less serviceable mind. Because they are the most sincere of lovers, and most loving of liars. They love with a passion and they lie with double. They're the most addictive of charmers. The crackheads of romance. The Escobar of flirt.

✦

So leave it to archetypical Italian, the southern variant, to show the why and how Cinema can be the most addictive of passions, in the most sincere of ways. That is director Giuseppe Tornatore's mission. To show both his censored and uncensored love for cinema. A shared love and a love that is to be shared. For this is the nature of Cinema. A beautified mirror of life. An extension of the mind and the eye.

An extension of the self, mirrored by the reality of the fictitious protagonist, Salvatore. At present day an affirmed movie director, Salvatore reminisces his times of infancy. Here we are, in a tiny southern Italian town, romanticized by the modern eye, but true to the condition of the past, ravaged by the post-World War II diaspora. Fatherless for a minute, Salvatore finds the comfort that is lacking in his life by sneaking into the local Cinema, Paradiso.

✦

Run by the only catholic priest that shoos little boys away from his laps, Salvatore develops a mutual friendship, and mutual mentorship, with the local projectionist, Alfredo. Alfredo guides him through the world of nitrate film, with nothing but a firm hand on the projector. But when the time comes, and the body comes lacking, Alfredo must ask to be guided by a grown Salvatore, in exchange for his continued mentorship.

It is now that the true mentorship begins. A mentorship of life, sweetened by the periodical metaphors brought to our screen by his own life spent in front of the big screen. All those years of projecting, cherished by Alfredo in his life as a cinephile, these can finally be projected to Salvatore as paternal guidance. That is the truth of Cinema. An expedient to highlight the reality of life, briefly shown in between a long alternation of shots; captured by the experiencing mind and elaborated by the experienced.

✦

Tornatore's classic is thus a nostalgic dream for the old, and a life-affirming adventure for the new. Nothing too fancy. No extravagance here. Tornatore's honesty lies in avoiding technical pursuits beyond his abilities, or duties, critical to showcase his sincere love for this art. An uncompromised love, if not by the duties of life.

But it is along those duties that we are briefly reminded of what makes it all worth living, so that we can love all over again.
