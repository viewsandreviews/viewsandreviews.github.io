Film Review

Monkey Man

Good Monke
Director Dev Patel
Year 2024
Starring Dev Patel, Sharlto Copley, Pitobash

A promising career as an unseasoned action man,
but much more seasoned man.
Cardamom, mostly.

From the guy who played a poor Indian boy with mommy issues — in not one, not two, not five, but a grand total of three films now — here comes actor, director, part-time screenwriter and seldom Bollywood dancer Dev Patel. Now also ripped action star, and the closest thing to a heartthrob that India was able to export into the Western World, with no small thanks to a borrowed British accent.

The Dev is at his debut on many fronts, and he makes sure to make all count — minus parts of an oversaturated vendetta story.

✦

There he goes, back to the motherland — the one that kickstarted his career — to do much of the same and kickstart a new one. He is now in India, as a boy who has grown into a man, to reach the final stage of all evolutions: the monkey man.

Starting as but a small boy like millions of others around the vast Indian plains. One that comes from the villages — much more protagonist than any other setting found in the subcontinent. He reminisces of the violence that signed his childhood and adolescence, as flashes of trauma are the main motives for his revenge mission.

Who he needs to enact this vengeance against? Everyone, pretty much — as everyone falls into that vast category of corrupt power-hoarders that control the lives of the locals. From corrupt policemen to corrupt yogi politicians. Everyone must pay, including any unfortunate cook who came to work on an unfortunate shift.

✦

While the first half of the movie feels more like a repossession and redistribution of borrowed action scenes and clichés, Patel reconnects to the traditions of the land as the minutes pass. What could have been an almost missed opportunity is now a semi-fleshed-out and professional blend of Indian spices, Southeast Asian somersaults, and Western modern classics.

The action is protagonist from the very beginning, and it's nothing short of superb. A clever combination of choices allows for an acceptable amount of realistic extravagance, building up his monkey character along the way. While the film may follow an ever-increasing line of John Wick-wannabes, the camerawork and excellent choreography are more reminiscent of the Tony Jaa classics and modern East Asian success.

✦

A broth of violence and mixture of backgrounds sets the tone for the all-out action, until an overdue pause comes to reinvigorate the story. Modern political commentary of a region populated by most but known by few is intertwined with an exploration of subcultures — although the Hollywood overstress of inclusion is at times more forced than welcomed.

The last minus is a soundtrack that for sure keeps the blood pumping, but also the nerves uneased, owing to an overdose of modernised Eye of the Tiger moments.

That said, Monkey Man earns the highest stamp of Good Monke, for he is indeed a good monke. Good Monke.
