Personality Profile

Alexander Payne

The Acquaintance

He travels that America,
sharing the backseat with us —
bringing a nostalgic kindness akin to the greats of Asian cinema.

I realise just now, as I type on this laptop that ought to be used for shareholder satisfaction purposes, that I never took the time to talk about a director in its fullness. Here I am, running to remedy. Maybe not run — sprint it will be. As sprints are those brief, sporadic efforts I have made to discuss the role that attracts me the most to cinema, bar JAV cameraman.

Passion? But of course. Professionality? 100%. Clothing? Optional.

That said, I will start with a name that will ring very few bells. Fact is, it just recently rang my own — although it had been an acquaintance for a long time. That is an apt description of Alexander Payne. The acquaintance.

✦

The Nebraska native is one of the many acclaimed directors who remains under the radar to the masses. One whom you may have accidentally met in a past decision of mature movie viewing, but also one not to have forced himself on you to the point of required inquisition. That is the Payne. One who loves to talk about that unglamorous America he breathed through for most of his life — America that he makes shine, without ever overstepping into the background.

His road trip movies leave space for voyages in all kinds of American lands, filled with encounters that can often teach a thing or two. He travels that America, sharing the backseat with us; bringing a nostalgic kindness akin to the greats of Asian cinema. It is the voices of the often left behind that are now explored, without judgement or pity.

✦

These are not marginalised realities. These are instead often those noiseless majorities that inhabit many environments — victims of time and unspoken rules of society. No distinction of class to be made when exploring their faults or persecutions. All of his characters are vastly different in creed and austerity. Unique; only sharing those lumps of suffering, often caused by others who also suffer. For there is always a bigger fish.

Both of his collaborations with Paul Giamatti bring out the shine in the bald-headed prodigy. The first entry, Sideways, ignites the actor's full versatility, while the most recent in The Holdovers brings him back to a past of high comedy. High on life and spirit — those are the requirements for a Payne performance. Because it's life, often his own, that Payne wants to talk about. A life he cherishes. One where clouds often obfuscate a sky that never seems unreachable. One that often opens up for rays of sunshine.

✦

Flawed, hurt, middle-aged men are Payne's speciality. Broken fatherly figures who have to keep it together because that is what's needed. For this, his work in Nebraska remains his most acclaimed and most touching showcase. It is also his one attempt at discolouring the world, with black and white becoming the palette of his lost hometown. As colours fade away, one may struggle to find joy in this rural world — never as simple as it is made out to be, if one does not look attentively enough. Those little moments may be hard to notice, but their value is slowly unveiled and made fully clear in his final acts.

His kind touch does leave room for criticism, as it sometimes seems to devolve into less demanding attitudes toward his collaborators. This leniency is at times transformed into moments of awkward opaqueness, be it in acting performances or directing.

But in the end, it will still be his humanistic approach to his craft that Payne will be remembered for — Me Too indiscretions notwithstanding.
