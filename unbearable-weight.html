Film Review

The Unbearable Weight of Massive Talent

The Cage Fanboy Speaks
Director Tom Gormican
Year 2022
Starring Nicolas Cage, Pedro Pascal, Tiffany Haddish

The laments of an unsatisfiable groupie,
whose dreams of a world full of bee fighting,
skull flaming, vampires in bear costumes
is a preposterous delusion of Utopia.

As movie producers are tasked with the ever more challenging duty of finding untaken domain names to publicize their next move, let me continue this series of obnoxiously long titled feature length film reviews. This time with my most anticipated film for this slumpy 2022, as the inner Cage fanboy in me screamed in adoration for this whole three month long wait.

Admittedly, I have approached TUWOMT with great fears. Fear of one more failed attempt at capturing the King of Cringe's massive talent, with unbearable... weight?

This was exacerbated further after the first clips of TUWOMT started rolling out and about, showing it break the first and only rule of meme-club: never make memes self-aware. As shown by the hilarious attempt at capitalizing with Morbius-rage, memes and production teams have a lousy history of failure and cringe. When even Nickelback fans start goofing on you, you know it ain't morbing time no more.

✦

But when the one and only Nic is at its core humanity's greatest story of repeated failure and cringe, maybe, just maybe things might work out. And indeed, they did just work, albeit barely so.

The movie starts with what many of us have been fetishizing for decades to this date: Nicolas Kim Coppola in the role of a lifetime, Sir Nickelodeon McCagey — albeit a disappointingly restrained version of his cage-self. It then continues with part-time John Travolta in the not so clever trope of an improvised spy tackled with an even less clever rescue mission.

✦

The movie acts as a serviceable marriage device between the much mainstream comedy expected from the likes of Judd Apatow and all his disciples, together with enough self-consciousness to put on display Nick's repertoire of idiosyncrasies.

Many of its most joyously Cagey moments are in fact the ones where the writers commit to an in-detail breakdown of what is to be made for an ideal Cage movie, but failing to conclude that there is no such thing and the Cage is his own biggest oxymoron of freedom.

✦

Ending with the sole positive note of not being added to the infinite list of rotten movies starring the titular talent, I hope you do not mistake my avalanche of criticism for absolute dismissal of this adequate flick. As it is naught but the laments of an unsatisfiable groupie, whose dreams of a world full of bee fighting, skull flaming, vampires in bear costumes is a preposterous delusion of Utopia.
